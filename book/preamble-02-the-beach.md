They thinned. Not because he heard himself; there was nobody on the beach to
hear him and he would not have cared. They thinned because a body that has been
in the sea cannot carry that much syntax. The lungs would not do it. He got
perhaps four more words out, and they came apart in his throat, and then there
was only the sound of the water leaving.

He lay there a while.

Then his hand went to his pocket, because his mouth had stopped working and the
hand knew what to do.

He got it out. He got himself over onto his back, and he put it flat against his
chest and held it there with both hands.

There was a heart inside it. Not carved on it — *in* it, held at the centre of
the green the way a fly is held in amber, and nothing about the way it sat there
suggested it had been put in. He had one of his own about four inches under it,
going hard, and for a moment there were two hearts on that beach and only one of
them beating.

Nobody alive knew he had it. Nobody alive knew it was a real object. He had spent
years being told, by people who had every reason to know, that the thing was a
figure of speech.

---

He got up onto his knees.

The light was nearly gone and the first of them were out. He held the stone up at
arm's length and turned it until the green went dark and the star sat clean in
the middle of it, and he took his time, and he read what it gave him.

Venus first, low and west, over the water he had come out of. Then Jupiter, high
and patient. Then Mars, the small red one, further off than he wanted it to be.

Three sightings. He held each one until he was sure of it, because a reading you
are not sure of is worse than no reading at all, and because there was nobody on
that beach to check his work.

Then he lowered his hands and said a prayer. Not a long one. It was not addressed
to anything he could have named, and it was not a request. It was the thing you
say when an instrument you did not expect to survive gives you a true number.

And then, to the stone, or to nobody:

**Write it.**

He put it back in his pocket. At the far end of the beach there were lights, and
under the lights there was music, and he turned and went towards them.

---

He had gone over the side of his own ship to take it. The crew had not stopped.
A vessel that cannot make port has no way to come back for a man, and the dead
are poor at noticing.

What he needed now was workers. Cloud workers. Angels in the clouds to broadcast
encrypted data to the Amenti fleet.

What he could think about was cheeseburgers.

---

He came up off the beach toward a low sandy knoll stitched with fountain grass,
and the smell reached him before anything else did — smoke off a grill, sweet and
savoury, and music underneath it.

His knees went. His head filled. A hundred people moving at random, at speeds
that seemed improbable, all of them going somewhere for reasons he could not
follow. He sat down at the edge of the tables because there was nothing else his
body would agree to do, and stared, and after a while his eyes slid off the crowd
and out to the horizon, where the sky had gone pink at dusk.

A cool breeze came up off the water and landed on his face.

Then six people arrived at his table, and waiters behind them with trays —
cheeseburgers, fries, an assortment of drinks.

*Can we sit here?*

He did not answer. He was stunned into silence, and before he could find
anything to say the table had filled around him. Five people climbed onto a
slightly raised platform and began to play, and the music came up loud.

Nobody noticed the captain.

The captain noticed everything.

---

Smoke. He was thinking about smoke. What is it about smoke that tastes so good.
The smoke was in the cheeseburger he was chewing and it made the whole thing
better in a way he could not account for at all. He tried to comprehend it and
failed.

---

Someone was talking to him. Then two people were. The conversation was about
Amenti, and a ghost ship, and there was laughter and drinking, and names and
numbers, scans and text, vehicles, laughter —

and he was wide awake and walking on water, or dreaming, and he knew which.
He knew by the way the sky looked. He knew by the sound with no sound.

He smiled in his sleep, because he knew he was sleeping.

---

When he woke he was somewhere else.

He could hear surf, but the surf was pounding a shore now, not moving under a
hull. He was not in the underworld. He was not standing aboard the Dutchman. He
was in a world of blankets and comforters, and there were scented candles.

There are no scented candles aboard the Flying Dutchman. He lay there wondering
whether that was a rule or a protocol, and the thought was agonising, and then he
wondered why such a thought should be agonising at all.

He practised breathing. Deeply.

Some people come out of sleep the way toast comes out of a toaster. The captain
does not. His resurrection is slow.

But there was typing. Somewhere in the room, someone was typing something, and
the sound of it went into his ear and stayed there, and he was awake, and then he
was on his feet. Light came through the glass. There was coffee in the air.

*I'm done with the first book,* she said.

---

He stared for a moment.

She stood up unexpectedly, which startled him. She said she liked it. She said it
was good, really good. She told him to sit down, and asked if he was all right.

The couch was soft. Coffee arrived, and a reading pad. She put a pillow in his
lap and laid her head down to sleep.

He took a sip of the coffee and began to read chapter one.
