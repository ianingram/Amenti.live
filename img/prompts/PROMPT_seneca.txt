### SENECA THE YOUNGER — CARD
status:  SHIPPED

status:  AWAITING GENERATION — 9:16, 2K, polish ON. key: viewer's left

Seneca in the bath, ordered to die by his own pupil. A villa four miles outside Rome, AD 65. Sixty-eight years old, gaunt and sinewy, hollow-cheeked, hair thin and cut short, clean-shaven; an old man's body, spare and worn. SEATED UPRIGHT IN A LONG RECTANGULAR BATH, naked to the waterline, back straight against the end, both forearms resting along the rim, head up and level. THE BATH IS THE WEALTH: cut from a single block of WHITE MARBLE WITH GOLD-BROWN VEINING, its rim and inner lip sheathed in POLISHED GOLD, gold lion-mask spouts at the head end, a gold step down into it. The room around it is a private bath-hall in white marble: veined marble walls and floor, massy fluted columns, a gold basin on a tripod, silver flasks and a silver strigil laid on a marble ledge, folded white linen. HOT WATER TO HIS CHEST, and heavy STEAM rising off the surface and hanging in the room, softening everything beyond a few feet. IN THE CLEAR WATER, ONE SLOW THREAD OF BLOOD — a thin dark ribbon uncoiling from below the surface and clouding faintly as it spreads. That is all. No wound is visible, the water is not red, and nothing else in the frame is disturbed. Two attendants stand well back at the edge of the steam, still, hands at their sides, not intervening; a secretary a little nearer has a wax tablet and stylus and is WRITING — Seneca is still dictating. KEY LIGHT FROM THE VIEWER'S LEFT: hard low light from a high window falling in one shaft through the steam onto the water surface and across his chest and face, blazing on wet marble and wet gold; the far side of the hall falls to true black. camera at water level from the foot of the bath, a few paces off. FOUR GUARDS. ONE, HE IS OLD AND WORN — sixty-eight, gaunt, sinewy, hollow-cheeked, the neck corded, hair thin and receding. Tacitus says an austere diet had wasted his body. NOT a robust senator, NOT heroic, NOT youthful. TWO, HE IS A FIRST-CENTURY ROMAN — clean-shaven, hair cut short and forward. NO beard, no Greek philosopher type, no toga on the bath plate. THREE, NOTHING GRAPHIC. No wounds in frame, no cuts shown, no red water, no gore, no violence in progress. FOUR, NO DRAMA IN THE FACE — he is composed and level throughout. The whole point of the account is that he stayed calm and went on dictating. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. The tub and the man fill the lower two-thirds, cropped at the near rim so the water surface reads large. He sits centred and upright in it. Above and behind, the marble wall and the steam give the top third air. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. Expression COMPOSED AND CLEAR — level, unafraid, faintly impatient. Tacitus records that he went on dictating to the end. No agony, no grimace, no drama. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.

### SENECA THE YOUNGER — TERMINAL

status:  AWAITING GENERATION — 9:16, 2K, polish ON. key: viewer's right

Seneca writing On the Shortness of Life, surrounded by everything he says is worthless. His house in Rome, about AD 62, while he is still the richest private man in the empire and still Nero's minister. Sixty-five, gaunt and sinewy, clean-shaven, hair thin and cut short, in a plain white wool tunic with a narrow purple stripe — the ONLY plain thing in the picture. SEATED OFF TO ONE SIDE AT A WRITING TABLE CUT FROM WHITE MARBLE WITH DEEP GOLD-BROWN VEINING, its edge inlaid with a gold meander and its legs carved as gilt-bronze lion supports. On it: wax tablets in gilt frames, a silver stylus case, a gold inkwell, papyrus rolls in silver capsae, a small gold lamp of three wicks. One hand flat on an open tablet, the other cutting a line, head down, unaware of the room. THE ROOM IS THE WEALTH AND IT CARRIES THE FRAME: a long high hall of white marble, MASSY FLUTED COLUMNS of veined marble running away down both sides and up out of frame with gilded capitals, a coffered ceiling picked out in gold, walls panelled in coloured marbles, a mosaic floor, gilt-bronze lamp stands, silver vessels on a marble console, a bronze-and-gold water clock. ATTENDANTS WAITING: four or five household slaves standing motionless in the middle distance and along the colonnade — one with a folded cloak over his arm, one with a silver ewer and basin, two simply standing, all of them still and patient and looking at nothing, waiting to be needed and not being needed. KEY LIGHT FROM THE VIEWER'S RIGHT: the three-wick lamp on the table, low and close and warm, blazing on the gold inlay and the wet ink and the polished marble beside his hand, and falling off fast so the far columns and the far end of the hall drop to true black; the waiting attendants stand at the edge of it, half lit. Cold air; lamp smoke rising through the key. camera at table height, well back down the hall, so the marble and the columns read before the man does. FOUR GUARDS.
