### MOSES — CARD
status:  SHIPPED — art_sat set to 0.75 by hand

key: BEHIND (moon), reading as viewer's centre   ·   seed ________  <- PASTE AT GENERATION TIME

THE FACE IS DARK ON PURPOSE. That is unusual for a card, and it works here for the
reason the Gandhi hooded plate works: every surface in the product prints the figure's
name in text — .rc-name, .cdx-row-name, .term-char-name, .term-bar-name — so the plate
never has to identify anyone. It is also the honest answer for a man of whom no
likeness survives.

BACKLIGHT IS WHAT KEEPS THE INVARIANT. One dominant source, hard-edged, shadow side to
true black — a figure standing in front of the moon satisfies all of that literally.

COLOUR IS CONFINED TO THE FIRE FAMILY. The blood moon and the altar fires are the same
hue and they are the only warm thing in frame; everything else is monochrome. Caesar's
card ships at accent 0.312, so the deck has room for this.

Moses standing against a setting blood moon. Bronze Age wilderness, the hour before dawn. Heavy-set and thick through the chest, broad-shouldered, jowled, still powerful at eighty. Wearing a coarse wool robe hanging in heavy slabs, a striped mantle across one shoulder lifting in the wind, an oiled leather belt, sandals; a long staff shod in bronze held upright in one fist, planted, his whole weight settled on it. BEHIND HIM AND LOW ON THE HORIZON, AN ENORMOUS SETTING FULL MOON — filling a third of the sky, deep and almost blood-red, its disc sitting on the far ridge. HE STANDS BETWEEN US AND IT, so he is a near-silhouette: the moon is the only key and it comes from directly behind, throwing a hard bright rim down the edge of the robe, along the shoulder, through the wind-lifted hair and beard, and down the length of the staff, while the whole front of him — face included — falls to true black. THE FACE IS OBSCURED AND DARK, read only as a shape. Far off across the plain, SEVERAL SACRIFICIAL ALTARS BURNING on the high places, small and low, each one a point of fire under its own column of smoke, the smoke rising and drifting flat across the face of the moon. Wind-driven grit and ash across the frame; the dust in the air lit red where it crosses the disc. Bare stony ground, cracked and dark, running away to the ridge. camera low, at knee height, looking up so he stands over the horizon and the moon sits behind his shoulder. NO HORNS. Moses is never horned here — that is a Vulgate mistranslation of keren, and models reach for Michelangelo by default. NOT a fair-skinned European patriarch: a Levantine man raised in Egypt, sun-dark, weathered, of the period and region. No authentic likeness survives, so render a characteristic face rather than a portrait — and here it is in shadow in any case. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic character plate: the figure, the ground and the sky are MONOCHROME, near black-and-white. The ONLY colour anywhere is the deep blood-red of the moon and the distant altar fires — one warm family, one hue, and nothing else in the frame is warm. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Full figure standing, centred, dark against the moon, cropped at the shin. The head sits high in the top third with the moon behind and below it and open sky above. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. The face is IN SHADOW and largely unreadable — a suggestion of brow, nose and beard found by rim light only. No eye highlight. Identity is carried by silhouette and staff. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.

### MOSES — TERMINAL
status:  SHIPPED — accent 0.036, the cleanest reading of the invariant in the deck

key: viewer's right   ·   seed ________  <- PASTE AT GENERATION TIME

Moses alone on the summit of Nebo at first light, at the very end. The plains of Moab, Bronze Age. A hundred and twenty years old — deeply weathered, hollowed, the beard long and white, but the frame still heavy and the back still straight; his eye was not dim nor his natural force abated. Wearing a plain coarse wool robe, dust-coloured and worn thin, a striped mantle pulled across the shoulders against the cold, an oiled leather belt, sandals; the long bronze-shod staff planted in the rock beside him, one hand resting on it. Standing at the very edge of the ridge with his back three-quarters to us, LOOKING OUT AND DOWN over a wide river valley — a pale river winding, dark groves, a walled town far off, hill country beyond it running to the horizon. He is not entering it. A SETTING FULL MOON low over the far hills, cold and clear, and the first grey of dawn behind him; the moon is the key, hard and raking from the viewer's right, catching the wind-blown beard, the edge of the staff, and the rim of every rock. Cold night mist lying in the valley two stops down. Dew on the rock throwing hard points of light. camera low and well back, at knee height from behind and to one side, so the sky takes most of the frame. NO HORNS. Moses is never horned here — that is a Vulgate mistranslation of keren, and models reach for Michelangelo by default. NOT a fair-skinned European patriarch: a Levantine man raised in Egypt, sun-dark, weathered, of the period and region. No authentic likeness survives, so render a characteristic face rather than a portrait. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Full figure standing, small against the ridge and the sky, the land reading as much as the man. Subject in three-quarter profile turned away toward the valley, wholly unaware of being looked at. Great empty air above him and out to the horizon. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. Expression absorbed and unguarded, attention entirely on the country below. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.
