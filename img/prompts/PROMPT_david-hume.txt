### DAVID HUME — CARD
status:  SHIPPED — the guard landed first roll

key: viewer's left

David Hume outside the Advocates Library at night, Parliament Close, Edinburgh, about 1758, while he was its Keeper. Fifty and very stout — broad, thick-necked, heavy through the chest and belly, a large full plain face, double chin, small mild eyes, the mouth settled in something close to good humour. Wearing a plain drab-brown broadcloth coat with flat buttons, a long waistcoat straining slightly at the belly, white linen stock, a plain short bob-wig, black three-cornered hat under one arm, a stout walking stick in the other hand, a folio tucked under his elbow. Standing at the foot of the library door in the close: heavy Scots stonework, crow-stepped gables going up out of frame, a worn stone stair, an iron handrail, the great studded door standing half open behind him. Wet black flagstones from the drizzle, and sea haar and coal smoke lying in the close and catching the light. THE KEY IS THE LIBRARY ITSELF: tall astragal windows above and behind him burning warm with candlelight from inside, and the open door throwing a long warm wedge of light down the wet stone — the light comes from the viewer's left and rakes across him, and the shadow side falls to true black. Everything wet throws hard highlights: flagstones, iron, the shoulders of the coat, the stone. camera at chest height from a few paces down the close, looking slightly up. TWO THINGS THE MODEL WILL GET WRONG UNLESS TOLD. FIRST, HE IS FAT — broad, heavy, thick-necked, a large full face and a double chin, a big belly under the waistcoat. Do not slim him. SECOND, HE IS NOT A TORTURED GENIUS — the face is plain, placid, mild and faintly amused, and his contemporaries all remarked that it gave no outward sign of intelligence whatever. No brooding, no furrowed anguish, no romantic melancholy. He is the most cheerful man in this deck and he should look it. Plain brown or drab clothes and a plain short bob-wig, not an aristocrat's finery. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Waist-up, cropped just below the belt. Subject centred, facing the viewer, shoulders filling the width of the frame. The head occupies the top third with a hand's width of space above it. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. ### DAVID HUME — TERMINAL
status:  SHIPPED with three knowing deviations: painted, faces to camera, Hume at half the frame

key: viewer's right

THE ADVOCATES LIBRARY IN EDINBURGH, ABOUT 1756, WITH ITS KEEPER AT WORK IN IT. This is a portrait of the room first and of the man second. A long high hall in dark oak: press after press of shelved books running away down both sides and up out of frame, a gallery with a turned balustrade above, a rolling ladder against one press, a globe on a stand, tall arched astragal windows down one wall. The shelves are dense with CALF AND MOROCCO BINDINGS TOOLED IN GOLD — banded spines, gilt lettering, gilt rules and corner-pieces catching the light in long receding rows, red and dark green and tan leather. On the tables the older stock: worn and rubbed brown calf gone soft at the corners, limp vellum, unbound quires tied with tape, a few chained folios, foxed paper. Broad tables strewn with open folios on book-rests held flat with lead weights, loose foolscap, quills, horn inkwells, a penknife. DAYLIGHT IS THE KEY — a single tall window on the viewer's right admitting one hard shaft of low afternoon sun that crosses the room, lights the dust in the air, strikes the gold tooling on one press so the spines blaze, and lays a bright bar across a table of open books; everything outside the shaft is two stops down and the far end of the hall falls to true black. Warm light, cold room. HUME IS OFF TO ONE SIDE, seated at a table, in coat and plain bob-wig, one hand flat on an open folio, the other lifted mid-word — DIRECTING A YOUNG CLERK who stands beside him holding a bundle of manuscripts tied with tape and waiting to be told where they go. Further off, two visitors in coats and hats stand at a press with their backs half turned, one reaching a book down; a second clerk on the ladder. Nobody looks at the camera. camera at table height, well back down the length of the hall, so the room reads before the man does. TWO THINGS THE MODEL WILL GET WRONG UNLESS TOLD. FIRST, HE IS FAT — broad, heavy, thick-necked, a large full face and a double chin, a big belly under the waistcoat. Do not slim him. SECOND, HE IS NOT A TORTURED GENIUS — the face is plain, placid, mild and faintly amused, and his contemporaries all remarked that it gave no outward sign of intelligence whatever. No brooding, no furrowed anguish, no romantic melancholy. He is the most cheerful man in this deck and he should look it. Plain brown or drab clothes and a plain short bob-wig, not an aristocrat's finery. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. A WIDE ROOM PORTRAIT. The library is the subject and Hume is in it — he sits OFF-CENTRE and occupies less than a third of the frame height, with the presses, the window and the length of the room taking the rest.
