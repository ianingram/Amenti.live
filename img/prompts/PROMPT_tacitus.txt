### TACITUS — CARD
status:  SHIPPED

key: viewer's right

Tacitus on the steps of the Curia at night, newly consul. Rome, AD 97. Forty-one years old, lean and dry, close-shaven, hair cut short and forward, a hard narrow mouth and the eyes of a man who has watched a great deal and said none of it. Wearing the SENATORIAL TOGA, heavy white wool wound over one shoulder and falling in deep folds, still WET FROM RAIN so the wool is dark, heavy and gleaming rather than dry and flat; beneath it a tunic with the broad purple stripe at the neck, a heavy gold ring on one hand, red leather senatorial boots. One arm gathering the toga at the waist, a closed bronze-cased scroll held against his side in the other hand. Behind him the bronze doors of the Curia, shut, and the wet Forum falling away into darkness — colonnade, standing statues, black between them. Rain just stopped; every marble step, every bronze fitting and the whole street sheeting with water and throwing hard highlights. Key light from the viewer's right, low and hard: a pitch torch in an iron bracket beside the doors, raking across the wet wool and the wet marble, the shadow side falling to true black. A low moon behind broken cloud far above the colonnade, small and cold, the only other light. camera at chest height from a few steps below, looking slightly up. NO AUTHENTIC LIKENESS OF TACITUS SURVIVES — no bust, no coin. Render a characteristic Roman senatorial face of the late first century rather than a portrait: lean, close-shaven, hair cut short and forward in the Trajanic manner, NOT bearded and NOT a Greek philosopher type. He is Gallo-Roman provincial gentry, not a Julio-Claudian aristocrat. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Waist-up, cropped just below the belt. Subject centred, facing the viewer, shoulders filling the width of the frame. The head occupies the top third with a hand's width of space above it. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. Expression composed and level, mouth closed, eyes catching a single sharp highlight. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.

### TACITUS — TERMINAL
status:  SHIPPED

key: viewer's left

Tacitus writing the Annals, alone at night. Rome, about AD 116, twenty years later. Sixty years old — the same lean dry face gone hollow, the neck corded, the hair thin and grey, still close-shaven. Stripped to a plain undyed wool tunic, no toga, sleeves pushed back, an old cloak over the chair behind him. Seated at a broad table CUTTING INTO A WAX TABLET with a bronze stylus, mid-word, hunched close over it. On the table: a hinged diptych of wax tablets, three or four opened papyrus rolls weighted flat with bronze, an inkpot, a second stylus, a shallow bronze oil lamp with two wicks burning. Behind him a wall of pigeonholed shelving stuffed with rolled scrolls going up into the dark, and — set on a plinth just at the edge of the light, half in shadow, facing him — a MARBLE PORTRAIT BUST OF TIBERIUS, the emperor whose reign he is writing, watching him work. Key light from the viewer's left, low and close, the two-wick lamp itself the source, falling off fast across the table so the far wall and the shelving drop to true black; the bust catches just enough of it to read. The polished wax, the bronze stylus, the lamp and the marble carry every highlight. Cold night air; the lamp smoke rising and catching the key. camera at table height from across the room, as if you had come in and stopped. NO AUTHENTIC LIKENESS OF TACITUS SURVIVES — no bust, no coin. Render a characteristic Roman senatorial face of the late first century rather than a portrait: lean, close-shaven, hair cut short and forward in the Trajanic manner, NOT bearded and NOT a Greek philosopher type. He is Gallo-Roman provincial gentry, not a Julio-Claudian aristocrat. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Full figure seated, placed off-centre with the room opening away behind him, the shelved scrolls and the bust reading as much as the man. Subject turned into his own work and not toward the viewer. Generous air above and beside him. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. Expression absorbed and unguarded, attention entirely on the line he is cutting. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.
