### SHAKA ZULU — CARD
status:  SHIPPED — second roll; the shield clause rewritten by size against a body part

status:  AWAITING GENERATION — 9:16, 2K, polish ON. key: viewer's left (the risen sun)

REPLACES the kwaBulawayo daylight version, which returned with a long thin throwing
assegai and a small round shield — the two objects that identify him, both defaulted
away from. The weapon clauses are now stated by SIZE AGAINST A BODY PART and by what
they are NOT, rather than by the words iklwa and isihlangu, which the model evidently
holds no picture for. That is the Gandhi lesson: six polite requests for small round
wire spectacles failed, and 'he is fat' landed first roll.

BUILT FOR DRAMA. Low camera looking up so he stands over the horizon. Waist-deep dawn
mist. The sun breaking the horizon behind him cutting hard bars of light between the
ranked shields. Regiments receding rank by rank until the furthest are only shapes.

Shaka kaSenzangakhona standing before his regiments at sunrise. kwaBulawayo, about 1824. Roughly forty, tall and heavily built through the chest and shoulders, deep brown-black skin, a broad strong face, hair cropped close, the skin OILED so it throws hard speculars down the shoulder and the length of the forearm. Wearing full Zulu royal regalia: a soft calfskin IBHESHU behind and an UMUTSHA of twisted genet tails in front, thick white cow-tail AMASHOBA bound above the elbows and below the knees, an otter-skin headband with a SINGLE TALL CRANE FEATHER standing straight up from it, a leopard-skin cloak hanging from one shoulder, brass rings and beadwork at the throat. He stands STILL AND SQUARE, weight even, chin level: the short broad-bladed spear held low and underhand at his hip, blade down, and the other hand resting on the top rim of the great oval shield standing on the ground beside him, its top at his chin. BEHIND HIM, RANK AFTER RANK OF HIS REGIMENTS RECEDING INTO MIST — hundreds of men in ordered files with their great oval shields held at rest making a long unbroken wall across the ground, the nearest sharp and each rank behind it fainter, until the furthest are only shapes. Beyond them the ring of the homestead: domed thatched dwellings and a split-pole palisade, half dissolved in the mist. THE MIST IS THE PICTURE. Heavy cold ground mist lying waist-deep across the beaten red earth at dawn, and THE SUN JUST BREAKING THE HORIZON BEHIND AND TO THE VIEWER'S LEFT — coming in almost horizontally, cutting GOD-RAYS through the mist and between the ranked shields in long hard bars of light and shadow, blazing white where it strikes the cow-tails and the oiled shoulder and the spear blade, and leaving everything else at true black. Dust and cook-smoke rising into the beams. Steam off the men. camera LOW, at knee height a few paces in front of him, looking steeply UP so he stands over the horizon with the feather against open sky and the ranks falling away behind him into the light. FIVE GUARDS. ONE, HE IS ZULU — deep brown-black skin, a broad strong face, close-cropped hair, tall and heavily muscled through the chest and shoulders. Roughly forty. TWO, THE REGALIA IS SPECIFIC AND IT IS NOT A LOINCLOTH: an IBHESHU of soft calfskin behind and an UMUTSHA of twisted fur tails in front, thick white cow-tail AMASHOBA bound above the elbows and below the knees, an otter-skin headband, a single tall crane feather, a leopard-skin cloak over one shoulder, brass and bead at the throat. NOT bones, NOT face paint, NOT a Hollywood chief. THREE, THE SPEAR IS SHORT. A stout haft NO LONGER THAN A FOREARM with a BROAD LEAF-SHAPED BLADE AS WIDE AS A HAND, gripped underhand at the hip like a knife, blade down. It is NOT long, NOT thin, NOT held overhead, NOT a throwing spear. THE SHIELD IS HUGE AND OVAL — a full cowhide reaching FROM HIS CHIN TO HIS KNEE, patterned black and white, NOT round and NOT small. Every warrior behind him carries the same short spear and the same great oval shield. FOUR, NO EUROPEANS, NO GUNS, NO PITH HELMETS, NO COLONIAL FRAMING — he is not being observed by anyone in this picture. FIVE, NO SNARLING. He is a working commander, not a monster; the monster is what Nathaniel Isaacs was paid to sell. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Full figure, centred, cropped at the shin, seen from BELOW so he stands over the camera and the crane feather breaks the top third against open sky. He fills the frame; the regiment and the mist go away behind him. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. Expression level, hard and assessing — a working commander looking at something that is his problem. Not snarling, not glowering, not theatrically fierce. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.

### SHAKA ZULU — TERMINAL

THE GREAT CATTLE ENCLOSURE AT KWABULAWAYO AT FIRST LIGHT, WITH THE KING SMALL IN IT. Zululand, about 1824. This is a portrait of the ground and the army first and of the man second. AN ENORMOUS CIRCULAR ENCLOSURE of beaten red earth, ringed all the way round by a palisade of split poles and by hundreds of domed thatched dwellings; the ring is so wide that the far side is small with distance. DRAWN UP ACROSS IT, A REGIMENT STANDING IN ORDERED RANKS — several hundred men in files, each with a large oval black-and-white ISIHLANGU shield held at rest and a short IKLWA upright beside it, the shields making a long unbroken patterned wall across the ground. Their cow-tail AMASHOBA and headbands catch the light. The ranks are still and silent; this is drill, not battle. SHAKA STANDS SMALL AND OFF TO ONE SIDE with his back three-quarters to us, out in front of the ranks — identifiable only by the leopard cloak and the single crane feather and by standing alone. Two attendants a little behind him. Cattle moving at the far side of the enclosure. HEAVY COLD GROUND MIST lies waist-deep across the whole enclosure at dawn, and THE SUN IS JUST BREAKING THE HORIZON at the viewer's right — coming in almost horizontally, cutting GOD-RAYS through the mist and between the ranked shields in long hard bars of light and shadow, throwing the shadow of every man in one direction across the red earth, blazing white on the cow-tails and the pale bands of the shields, and leaving the shadowed side of the ring at true black. Dust and cook-smoke banding the low light. The upper third of the frame is open sky, pale and cold, holding tone everywhere and blown out nowhere. camera low and well back inside the enclosure, looking along the ranks. FIVE GUARDS.
