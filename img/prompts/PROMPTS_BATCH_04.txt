EVERY PROMPT NOW LEADS WITH THE MONOCHROME CLAUSE. Caesar's card returned at
0.312 saturation with that clause buried at the end, after two hundred words
about polished bronze. Reading rooms are MORE prone to it — warm afternoon
light, wood panelling, brass, sunlit stone — so the three from batch 03 are
reissued here with the clause moved to the front. Use these, not those.

SUN TZU — the period discipline matters more here than anywhere. Almost every
image of Sun Tzu dresses a Spring and Autumn general in Ming dynasty robes,
two thousand years too late. The prompts specify Eastern Zhou lacquered
leather lamellar, a straight bronze jian rather than a curved sabre, and
bamboo writing slips rather than paper — paper did not exist yet. No likeness
survives, so both prompts ask for a characteristic face, not a portrait.

### SUN TZU — CARD

Black and white photograph. Monochrome, desaturated, no colour. Sun Tzu, author of The Art of War. Spring and Autumn period China, around 500 BC — the Eastern Zhou, NOT the Ming or Qing dynasty. No authentic likeness survives: render a characteristic face of the period and region rather than a portrait. A lean, spare man in his fifties, close-cropped beard, hair drawn up into a topknot under a dark cloth cap. Wearing lacquered leather lamellar armour of the Eastern Zhou — hundreds of small rectangular plates laced together in horizontal rows with silk cord, the lacquer black and glossy so every single plate throws its own hard highlight — over a heavy dark silk robe with wide sleeves. A straight double-edged bronze jian sword at his hip, NOT a curved sabre. In one hand he holds a bound bundle of bamboo writing slips, the strips narrow and cord-tied. Behind: the dark timber of a campaign tent, out of focus, fine dust in the light. Key light from the viewer's right, low and raking from the side, severe, a hard terminator down the centre of the face. Camera low, looking up so the figure towers. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. Every surface is lacquered, oiled or polished so it throws a hard specular highlight; nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Waist-up to three-quarter length, subject centred and facing the viewer, shoulders filling the width of the frame, head in the top third. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through. Expression composed and level, mouth closed. Photographic realism. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.

### SUN TZU — READING ROOM

Black and white photograph. Monochrome, desaturated, no colour. Sun Tzu alone at the end of the day, armour set aside. Spring and Autumn period China, around 500 BC — Eastern Zhou, NOT Ming or Qing. No authentic likeness survives: render a characteristic face of the period and region rather than a portrait. A lean, spare man in his fifties, close-cropped beard, topknot loosened. He wears only a plain undyed silk under-robe, sash-tied, sleeves pushed back from the forearms; the lacquered lamellar armour lies in a dark heap on a low stand beside him, still gleaming, the bronze jian laid across it. He sits cross-legged on a reed mat at a low writing table, half-turned, a long bundle of bamboo slips unrolled across the table under one hand. In the other he holds a brush over a stone ink slab. He is not writing — he has stopped, reading back what is already there. A single oil lamp on the table at the viewer's left is the only light: low, hard, and close, raking across the bamboo and the silk so every strip edge and every fold reads, catching the wet ink and the lacquer of the discarded armour in small hard speculars, and throwing his shadow long across the floor behind him. His face is lit from below and to the side, half in shadow, eyes down on the slips. The rest of the tent falls to near-black.
