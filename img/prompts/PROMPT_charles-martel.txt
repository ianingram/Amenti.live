### CHARLES MARTEL — CARD
status:  UNSHOT — the greyscale uphill version was generated and rejected

status:  AWAITING GENERATION — 9:16, 2K, polish ON. key: the whole sky

Charles Martel walking uphill beside his horse at the end of the day. Frankish Gaul, October, about 732. HE IS ON FOOT AND LEADING THE HORSE, not riding it — reins loose in one hand, walking at its shoulder, head down, the animal tired and blowing. A big plain warhorse, unarmoured, mud to the hocks, a rolled cloak and a round shield strapped behind the saddle. He is in a knee-length mail byrnie over wool, dulled and dark and mud-marked, a conical helm carried under the other arm, cropped hair, cross-gartered legs, a long straight sword at the hip. A FEW FOLLOWERS STRUNG OUT BEHIND HIM ON THE TRACK — five or six, no more, spread out and walking, one leading a second horse, one with a spear across his shoulder, all of them small in the frame and none of them looking at anything. Nobody speaks. THE HILL IS THE SUBJECT. A long bare grassed ridge rising across the frame, the track cutting up it in a pale line, coarse tussock and outcrops of stone, a few bare wind-bent thorns on the crest. Beyond and below, glimpsed country going away flat into haze. THE SKY IS A HIGH BRIGHT FLAT GREY and it takes THE UPPER HALF OF THE FRAME — heavy even overcast, luminous rather than dark, holding tone right across with no blown white anywhere. The figures read as near-silhouettes against it, and it is the brightest thing in the picture by a long way. Cold, still air. Breath from the men and the horse. Thin haze in the low ground. Light comes from the whole sky at once rather than from any one point — a hard bright overcast that lays a clean bright line along the top of every shoulder, the horse's back, the helm and the ridge itself, while everything facing the camera and everything below the skyline falls away dark. camera LOW and WELL BACK down the track, at knee height, looking UP the slope after them. THREE GUARDS, and they exist only to stop the picture reading WRONG — the name is printed on every surface of this product, so nothing here has to identify him. ONE, NO HAMMER. 'Martel' is a nickname given after his death; he never carried one, and the model will hand him a warhammer on the strength of the name alone. TWO, THIS IS 732 AND NOT THE HIGH MIDDLE AGES: NO plate armour, NO visored helm, NO heraldry, NO surcoat, NO crusader cross, NO crown. Plain wool, a mail byrnie, a conical helm with a nasal bar, a round shield, cross-gartered legs. THREE, HE IS NOT A VIKING — no horns, no furs, no braids. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic character plate in TRUE GREYSCALE. NO COLOUR ANYWHERE — no warm accent, no tint on metal, no cast in the light. A silver-gelatin photograph: neutral grey throughout, from bright paper-white sky to true black. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. GIVE IT AIR. Full figures placed LOW on the slope, small against the hill, with THE UPPER HALF OF THE FRAME OPEN SKY. He does not fill the frame. The hill, the sky and the distance are the picture. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. The hill and the sky are IN FOCUS. Depth comes from haze down the length of the country, not from blur. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. Skin has pores, scars and a faint sheen. Too far off for expression. He reads as a man walking uphill at the end of a long day, leading a tired horse. Photographic realism. Materials behave like real materials under a hard light. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.

THE CHURCH IS DELIBERATELY UNREADABLE. An earlier draft made this a room portrait —
arches, whitewash, a reliquary going out on a shoulder, three monks against the wall.
That is the Hume-library treatment and it is wrong here: the architecture would take
the frame and the man would become staffage. He is the picture. The room falls three
stops down to shapeless dark and the prompt says outright: do not describe the building.

THE SWORD ON THE TABLE DOES THE WORK. Drawn, laid flat, bare, blade toward the viewer,
beside one sheet of vellum with the seal still hanging. A man who has just signed
something, with the reason it will be enforced lying next to it. Nothing else on the
table, and the emptiness is the point.

TRUE GREYSCALE, matching the card. Precedent: frederick-douglass-terminal ships at

Charles Martel alone at a table, having just signed away the church's land. Frankish Gaul, about 735. Gibbon's chapter on him is titled for this: the saviour condemned. He took ecclesiastical estates to pay the men who had held the line at Tours, and the church cursed him for it. HE FILLS THE FRAME AND NOTHING ELSE IN IT MATTERS. Mid-forties, heavy through the neck and shoulders, a broad weathered face, cropped hair, a few days of beard, out of armour in a plain dark wool tunic with the neck open. He sits square to a plain heavy trestle table, forearms on the boards, HANDS STILL — one flat, one loosely closed. Not writing. Just sat there. ON THE TABLE, CLOSE AND LARGE: HIS SWORD, laid flat across the boards in front of him, drawn and lying bare with the blade toward the viewer — a long straight double-edged blade, worn, with a short simple crossguard and a plain heavy pommel. Beside it a single sheet of stiff vellum with a wax seal hanging from a cord, the wax still soft-looking; a stub of candle; nothing else. The table is otherwise empty and that emptiness is deliberate. BEHIND HIM THERE IS ALMOST NOTHING TO SEE. He is in a church, but the church is unreadable — the room falls away into flat cold darkness three stops down, no arch, no column, no altar, no detail a viewer could put a name to. Only the faintest suggestion of stone and of depth. Do not describe the building. KEY LIGHT FROM THE VIEWER'S LEFT, low and hard and cold: one shaft of grey daylight from a small high opening out of frame, falling across the table, across the bare blade so it runs a hard bright line down the steel, across the vellum and the seal, and up one side of his face — leaving the other side of the face and the whole room behind him at true black. Cold stone air; dust turning in the shaft; his breath faintly visible. camera at table height, close in, as if you had sat down opposite him and he had not looked up for a while. THREE GUARDS, and they exist only to stop the picture reading WRONG — the name is printed on every surface of this product, so nothing here has to identify him. ONE, NO HAMMER. 'Martel' is a nickname given after his death; the model will hand him a warhammer on the strength of the name alone. TWO, THIS IS 732 AND NOT THE HIGH MIDDLE AGES: NO plate armour, NO visored helm, NO heraldry, NO surcoat, NO crusader cross, NO crown, NO cross-hilted knightly greatsword. His sword is a LONG STRAIGHT DOUBLE-EDGED BLADE with a short simple crossguard and a plain heavy pommel. THREE, HE IS NOT A VIKING — no horns, no furs, no braids. NO COLOUR ANYWHERE — no warm accent, no tint on metal, no cast in the light. A silver-gelatin photograph: neutral grey throughout. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light.
