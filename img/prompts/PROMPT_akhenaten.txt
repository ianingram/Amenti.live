### AKHENATEN — CARD
status:  SHIPPED — the guard failed on every count; first plate ever to fail value_span

status:  AWAITING GENERATION — 9:16, 2K, polish ON. key: viewer's right

Akhenaten in the open court of the Great Temple of the Aten at Akhetaten, at sunrise. Egypt, about 1345 BC. In his early thirties. Rendered exactly as the Amarna sculptors rendered him: a long narrow elongated skull, long thin neck, heavy drooping jaw and pointed chin, thick full lips, narrow heavy-lidded eyes, hollow cheeks — and a body to match, with narrow sloping shoulders, thin arms, a soft chest, a swollen belly and wide heavy hips. Warm red-brown skin, clean-shaven. Wearing the BLUE KHEPRESH CROWN with a gold uraeus cobra at the brow, a broad gold-and-faience pectoral collar over the bare chest, a pleated white linen kilt of very fine translucent cloth belted with a gold sash, gold armlets and bracelets, sandals. Both arms held down and slightly forward, palms open and turned up. Standing at the foot of a low white limestone altar heaped with offerings — bread, fruit, lotus, a fired duck, jars — on a clean stone pavement. Around and behind him the court runs away: plain white plastered walls, square painted pillars, low altars in rows, no roof anywhere. HIGH ABOVE AND BEHIND HIM, THE ATEN: a plain sun disc just risen, with long straight rays coming down from it and EACH RAY ENDING IN A SMALL HUMAN HAND, several of the hands holding an ankh out toward him. KEY LIGHT FROM THE VIEWER'S RIGHT: hard low horizontal sunrise light coming flat across the court, blazing on the white limestone and the gold and the linen and striking him full down one side, while the shadow side of the body and the depth of the court fall to true black. Fine desert dust in the air lit through. camera at chest height a few paces back, slightly low. FIVE GUARDS, AND THE FIRST ONE INVERTS THE USUAL RULE. Every other figure in this deck has no surviving likeness. AKHENATEN HAS HUNDREDS, AND THEY ARE DELIBERATELY STRANGE — DO NOT NORMALISE HIM. Follow the Amarna sculpture exactly: a LONG NARROW ELONGATED SKULL, a long thin neck, a heavy DROOPING JAW and pointed chin, THICK FULL LIPS, narrow slanted heavy-lidded eyes, hollow cheeks. And the body with it: NARROW SLOPING SHOULDERS, thin arms, a SOFT CHEST WITH PRONOUNCED BREASTS, a swollen rounded BELLY, WIDE HEAVY HIPS AND THIGHS, thin calves. He is NOT a handsome conventional pharaoh, NOT a muscular warrior king, NOT a Tutankhamun type, NOT Hollywood Egyptian. The strangeness is the likeness. TWO, THE ATEN IS A PLAIN SUN DISC WHOSE RAYS END IN SMALL HUMAN HANDS, some holding the ankh. That single detail is what says which pharaoh this is. THREE, NO OTHER GODS — no Amun, no Ra-headed falcon, no Anubis, no Horus, no animal-headed figures of any kind. He abolished them. FOUR, NO PYRAMIDS, NO SPHINX, NO CAMELS — the pyramids are a thousand years earlier and camels are not yet in Egypt. FIVE, HE IS EGYPTIAN — warm red-brown skin, clean-shaven, no natural beard. Visible atmosphere catches the key so the light reads as a physical presence in the air and separates the figure from the background. Cinematic monochrome character plate, near black-and-white with a single warm accent carried only on metal. EVERY SURFACE IS WET, OILED OR POLISHED so it throws a hard specular highlight — nothing matte, nothing that eats the light. Hard highlights run along every fold and edge; deep true blacks in between. One dominant key, hard-edged, with a clear terminator across the face and body; the shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held only by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Volume carried by continuous tonal turn. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. THREE-QUARTER LENGTH, cropped at mid-thigh — the body must read, since the body is the point. Subject centred and facing the viewer, the head high in the top third with open sky above it. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background is a dark, shallow-focus suggestion of place, two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons on the back of the hand. Headgear sits firmly ON the skull and casts a shadow onto the brow that binds it to the head. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through: brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw, temple. SUNRISE OVER THE GREAT TEMPLE OF THE ATEN AT AKHETARTEN, WITH THE KING SMALL IN IT. Egypt, about 1345 BC. Four of the six works in this room are the Great Hymn to the Aten and the fifth is the founding of this city, so this plate is the hymn: the temple at the moment the disc clears the eastern cliffs. This is a portrait of the court first and of the man second. AN ENORMOUS OPEN-AIR COURT WITH NO ROOF — that is the whole architectural idea, because the god is the sun and cannot be shut in. Long white plastered walls, square painted pillars in receding rows, and HUNDREDS OF SMALL WHITE LIMESTONE OFFERING ALTARS set out in ordered ranks across the pavement running away to the far wall, each heaped with bread, fruit and flowers, thin smoke rising from braziers among them. Beyond the far wall, the low desert cliffs of the eastern horizon. AKHENATEN STANDS SMALL, off-centre, with his back three-quarters to us, BOTH ARMS RAISED STRAIGHT UP AND OUT toward the disc, face lifted, in a pleated white linen kilt and the blue khepresh crown — recognisable by silhouette alone, the long skull and heavy hips unmistakable even at that size. A few figures with him: a queen and two small daughters, and priests standing back among the altars. Nobody looks at the camera.
