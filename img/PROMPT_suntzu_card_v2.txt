Near black-and-white photograph with ONE exception. Everything in the frame is neutral desaturated grey — skin, cloth, timber, leather, stone, sky — EXCEPT the gold, which keeps its full warm colour. The gold is the only colour in the picture and it must read clearly against the grey. Sun Tzu, author of The Art of War. Spring and Autumn period China, around 500 BC — the Eastern Zhou, NOT the Ming or Qing dynasty. No authentic likeness survives: render a characteristic face of the period and region rather than a portrait. A lean, spare man in his fifties, close-cropped beard, hair drawn up into a topknot under a dark cloth cap. Wearing lacquered leather lamellar armour of the Eastern Zhou — hundreds of small rectangular plates laced in horizontal rows with silk cord, the lacquer black and glossy so every plate throws its own hard highlight — over a heavy dark silk robe with wide sleeves. THE GOLD, and nowhere else: fine gold inlay worked into the pommel, guard and grip fittings of a straight double-edged bronze jian sword at his hip (NOT a curved sabre); gold inlay in the scabbard mounts and the throat fitting; a gold thread woven through the silk cord binding his cap and the lacing at his shoulders; and gilt characters on the bound bundle of bamboo writing slips he holds against his chest in both hands. These few gold elements are warm, bright and unmistakable. Everything else in the frame is grey. Behind: the dark timber of a campaign tent, out of focus, fine dust in the light. Key light from the viewer's right, LOW AND HARD AND RAKING FROM THE SIDE — a strong single source, severe, throwing a clear terminator down the centre of the face so one side of the head is lit and the other falls away into shadow. This is not soft or even light. Camera low, looking up so the figure towers. Cinematic character plate. Every surface is lacquered, oiled or polished so it throws a hard specular; nothing matte. The shadow side falls almost to black and dissolves into the ground, held by a thin rim of bounce. Full tonal range with small blown speculars. Fine film grain, deep vignette, high micro-contrast. Waist-up to three-quarter length, subject centred and facing the viewer, shoulders filling the width of the frame, head in the top third. Vertical format, aspect ratio 9:16. Background two stops below the figure. Both hands fully visible and clearly resolved on the bamboo slips — palm, finger mass, thumb, knuckles, tendons. Face weathered and specific, skull structure reading through. Expression composed and level, mouth closed. Photographic realism. Sharp on the face, softer toward the frame edge.
