# CAPTAIN'S LOG · 4–6 SEPTEMBER 2026
**Ingram Manor LLC · Amenti.live**

Two days that began with a handoff asking for geography and ended with a map
that holds four registers the ship did not have: where a soul was and **when**,
where they crossed, what stood on the ground, and how long an event went on
mattering. Plus a probe that opens the page instead of reading files, and a
second one that hunts rather than checks.

It also contains a great deal of the assistant being wrong, recorded in §V.
This session's version of that section is longer than usual, and the reason is
worth stating at the top: **almost every fault found in two days was in a join,
a label, or a reader's reach — and almost none was inside a register.**

## I · WHAT WAS FOUND

### THE MAP WAS DRAWING BIRTHPLACES AND CLAIMING ACTIVITY

The geography handoff settled the claim before anything was built: a soul's
position is *"principal place of activity, at honest granularity. **Not
birthplace**, because birthplace misleads for anyone who mattered elsewhere."*

Tested against twenty unambiguous figures, **nineteen were the birthplace.**
Newton in Lincolnshire, Einstein at Ulm, Marx at Trier, Curie in Warsaw, Mozart
at Salzburg. Not one worked there. Tested again against rulers, the same:
Constantine at Naissus, Justinian at Tauresium, Hadrian at Italica, Catherine at
Stettin.

**No audit caught it because every value is correct.** Newton *was* born in
Lincolnshire. `probe-geo` verified the coordinate, the Region column arbitrated
the namesake, the gazetteer hash matched. The register was truthful and the
label on it was not.

> **THE MAP WAS RIGHT ABOUT EVERY FACT AND WRONG ABOUT WHAT THE FACTS WERE.**

### CÓRDOBA, THREE TIMES IN ONE SESSION

Córdoba, Spain is at 37.89°N. Córdoba, Argentina is at 31.41°S and has four
times the people, so an unqualified lookup prefers it. Three times this project
put a twelfth-century Andalusian philosopher in South America — and three
different guards caught it, no two by the same mechanism.

The same shape recurred all week: **Marathon → Saint-Maximin-la-Sainte-Baume.
Waterloo → Austin, Texas. Carthage → Ohio. Thebes → Greece. Olympia →
Washington State. Bethlehem → Brazil. Nola → New Orleans. Falmouth → Portland,
Maine. Adams → Pulandian, China.** Every one refused rather than pinned.

### DERIVATION DOES NOT WORK FOR PLACES

`EVENTS.csv` held 536 dated events and no place column. Deriving one from the
names was tried and **measured**: it matched 29% and most matches were wrong —
*Trojan War* → a town in Texas called Date, *Plato founds the Academy* → a town
in Missouri called Plato. Meanwhile forty-five battlefields matched **nothing**
and would have vanished in silence while the register read as complete.

> **A GAZETTEER LISTS POPULATED PLACES. A BATTLEFIELD IS NOT ONE.**

Place is authored. 500 rows across two worksheets, by hand.

### AND THE ANOMALIES HUNT, WHICH WAS ASKED TO RETURN TEN TIMES ITS VALUE

A probe that verifies only returns what it was given. `probe-anomalies.mjs`
knows no rules and goes looking. First run:

- **33 probable duplicate souls.** Buddha / Gautama Buddha · Saint Paul / Paul
  the Apostle · Hernando Cortés / Hernán Cortés · Erwin Schrödinger twice, once
  with the umlaut and once without. **Every accented name in the roster is
  doubled.** A duplicated soul splits its mentions, its room and its
  connections, so every count above it is wrong.
- **~60 reigns entered as lifespans.** Most of the Han and Jin successions and
  the Japanese emperors — *Emperor Chong of Han, AD 144–145*. Mehmed II reads
  1444–1446, which is his first reign. Each would refuse a correct dated seat.
- **Three impossible builders**, one of them mine: the Colosseum built AD 80 by
  Vespasian, who died in 79. True to history, false as written.
- **Six cities with two names on one coordinate** — Constantinople/Istanbul,
  Leningrad/St Petersburg, Bombay/Mumbai, Hanseong/Seoul.
- **The roster's real shape:** AD 500–1400 holds 368 souls and **four rooms.**
  The medieval millennium is the thinnest-served on the ship.

## V · THE ASSISTANT'S OWN ERRORS

Recorded because the pattern is more useful than any instance.

**GUESSED AT STRUCTURES INSTEAD OF READING THEM. THREE TIMES IN ONE HOUR.**
`G` holds a list of candidates, not one record. Those records carried no country
code. `EVENT_SEATS` answers a different question with the same keys and holds
`cambridge` → Cambridge **Massachusetts**, added for the founding of Facebook —
it put Newton at Harvard. Each was one `grep` away.

**LAUNDERED A NUMBER I HAD CHOSEN.** Set an event's echo to 26 comet passes
because it landed near the end of the register, then described it as *"arrived
at rather than asserted."* The captain stopped on the phrase, which is how the
bug under it was found: a fixed count is a duration, and *still standing in it*
is not a duration. Given 26, **the four oldest events I most wanted burning had
already gone out** — Hammurabi extinguished in AD 374 — while the three-year-old
World Wide Web burned fine.

**REPORTED A DISCREPANCY THAT DID NOT EXIST.** Mis-parsed `LIBRARY.json`'s array
as an object and announced that two room registers disagreed, 55 against 52.
They did not. Caught by the captain noticing names that should not be in a list.

**BROKE THE SAME MARK THREE TIMES.** Jupiter's line: drawn at a quarter opacity
and reported as missing from the application; then absent past the register's
last rising because the guard refused invisibly; then a "fix" that explained the
absence instead of removing it. **The line is Giza's latitude and is true in
every year. Only the travelling sign is a count.** I had made the permanent
thing conditional on the passing one.

**WROTE A PROBE THAT COULD NOT BE READ.** Styled its output with `%c`; Safari
filed it under a hidden console category. The probe ran perfectly and printed
nothing a reader could see — the exact failure it was written to catch,
committed by the probe itself. Then wrote a second version that stopped at *"the
map did not mount"* without saying why, which is the same fault again.

**DECLARED FILES LANDED, OR NOT LANDED, ON A SINGLE STALE READ.** Repeatedly.
Most were caching; one was real and only settled by fetching through three
independent paths including GitHub Pages.

**LOST A REGISTER TO A FILENAME.** `GEO.json` was overwritten with a copy of
`amenti-map.js` during an upload. Valid JavaScript at a JSON path; every file
probe passed. Found only because the hall probe checks whether a register starts
with `{`.

**PRESENTED FILES WITHOUT A TREE.** Repeatedly, after agreeing not to. Six files
across three destinations with the placement line only after the last card.

## VI · THE LAWS THIS SESSION EARNED

> **A SPATIAL CLAIM SCALES WITH THE MAP. A TEMPORAL ONE DOES NOT.**
> Anything that refuses to scale cannot be read as kilometres. That single
> property is what makes an event's ring honest.

> **A BOUNDED NUMBER CANNOT CARRY AN UNBOUNDED CLAIM.**

> **A LINE MUST BE AUTHORED AS A JOURNEY, NEVER DERIVED FROM TWO SEATS.**

> **DO NOT MAKE A PERMANENT FACT CONDITIONAL ON A PASSING ONE.**

> **HOW HOT AND HOW LONG ARE DIFFERENT CLAIMS.** Tying them smuggles a second
> assertion in behind the first.

> **A DERIVED CLAIM THAT SHOWS ITS ARITHMETIC CAN BE CHECKED. ONE THAT OFFERS
> ONLY A DISCLAIMER CAN ONLY BE BELIEVED.**

> **A PROBE THAT ASKS A DIFFERENT QUESTION THAN THE REGISTER ANSWERS IS WORSE
> THAN NO PROBE.** It teaches a reader to ignore findings.

> **A PERMANENT HARMLESS ERROR IS WORSE THAN NONE.** It is the line a real
> failure hides behind.

> **A CORRECT REFUSAL THAT LOOKS IDENTICAL TO A BROKEN FEATURE IS A DESIGN
> FAULT.** Three times in one day.

> **A FEATURE THAT EXISTS, IS WIRED, AND CANNOT BE REACHED READS TO A VISITOR
> AS ABSENT — AND THAT IS WORSE THAN ABSENT, BECAUSE NOBODY LOOKS FOR IT
> TWICE.**

> **COUNT FIRST. THE APPEARANCE IS NOT THE EVIDENCE.**
