DILUTION DYNAMICS  ·  THE O SIGNAL

The price is right.
On your mark, get set, sell.

Right for them. A short squeeze is forced buying into constricted supply. Dilution is voluntary selling into expanded supply. Same mechanism, opposite sign — and the dilution side is the one announced in advance, on a form, by the person doing it. Fifty-nine experiments looked for an edge; this is the one that survived.

the thesis

When a company that recently escaped penny-stock status runs 50% in a month and then files a secondary offering, its managers are choosing to sell shares at that price. They can see the order book, the customer pipeline and the ramp schedule. You can see the filing.

Across 8,320 filings the drift after a 424B5 is −2.35% over 21 days, t = −4.77. Narrowed to this screen — uplisted, ran hard, overbought, then diluted — 117 deduplicated events gave −38.7% median over six months, negative in all five walk-forward windows.

The desk calls it the O signal — O for offering. It is the only thing in fifty-nine experiments that survived its own scrutiny.

ON THE NAME

The company is liquidating — converting its own equity into cash at a price it likes. That is the act the whole thesis turns on. Dilution describes what happens to you; liquidation describes what they are doing. The desk is named for the behaviour it watches.

Three of those events follow. They are the 2nd percentile, the median, and the worst.

SPY BUY  ·  SPY HOLD  ·  SPY SELL

Three verbs, one loop. The watching is constant; the acting is rare and comes in two halves that only work together.

SPY BUY
Not endorsed here. The corpus found no entry.
Fifty-nine probes produced no buy signal — every survivor is an exit or an avoidance. Probe 49 measured buying into the run and found half the decline with double the adverse move. And probe 55 measured the re-entry itself: bought twelve months after a signal, it returns a median −21.7% at one year and loses to the index on 70% of events, median excess −37.3 points. Probe 39 compared two ways of managing a position you already hold; it never tested the rebuy as a purchase.
SPY HOLD
The default, and almost always the answer.
The screen fires about thirty-five times a year across every listed company. On any given day, for any given ticker, the correct output is no. Thirty-four of the forty-nine rejected probes are records of what happens when this step is skipped — a pattern traded without first asking what it does to a random walk.
SPY SELL
When the screen fires on something you already hold.
The one route the evidence supports, and timing is most of it. Probe 57: selling at the filing day's close and sitting in cash beat holding by +37% at the median on 80% of 102 events, and the mean passes too (+22%, t 3.7). Half the value is gone by about 31 bars — probe 39's wait for the double top. No borrow, no margin, no expiry; the worst case is opportunity cost, and cash cannot margin-call you.

And the order is not optional. Buying without spying is every failed probe in the corpus. Spying without acting is a library. The one thing the forty-nine experiments produced that survives contact with a real account is the middle verb, and it only pays if the third one follows.

the mechanism

Equity is a currency a company prints. The higher its price, the less of the company each unit buys back in cash. That is the whole of it, and it decides everything else in this brief.

Suppose a company needs $400 million. At $10 a share it must issue 40 million shares. At $22.65 it issues 17.7 million. Same cash, 2.3× less of the company given away. No judgement about the business is required to prefer the second. Only a view about the price.

where the money comes from

It is not free and it is not new. The cash is real, but it is now divided across more claims on the same business. Issue 20% more shares and every existing holder's ownership falls by that proportion — the company is no richer per share, it simply has more shares.

the opposite of a squeeze

A short squeeze is forced buying into a supply that has been constricted. An offering is voluntary selling into a supply that has been expanded. Same mechanism, opposite sign — and only one of them is announced in advance, on a form, by the party doing it.

That is why this signal is different from everything else tested in this repository. Momentum, moving averages, chart patterns, planetary position, attention, short volume — every one of those is an attempt to infer intent from price. A 424B5 is the intent, filed. The float changes by a known amount, on a known date, at a price the issuer chose.

Supply shocks move price directly. That is not a behavioural claim or a statistical curiosity; it is the most basic mechanism a market has. A squeeze works because shares cannot be found. An offering works because they can suddenly be found in quantity, sold by the one participant who knows the company best and has decided this is a good price at which to part with it.

WHY THIS ONE AND NOT THE OTHERS

Fifty-nine experiments tested patterns, cycles, flows, attention, geometry and the sky. The one that survived is the one where the float itself changes. Everything else asks what price might be about to do. This asks what the supply of shares is about to be, and gets the answer from the filing.

why this makes it a signal

Now reverse the incentive. Management chooses the moment. They can see the order book, which design wins convert, whether the ramp lands in 2027 or 2029. If they believed the shares were cheap, selling them would be the one thing they would not do — you do not print currency you think is undervalued.

So the act of issuing is itself a statement, and it is the best-informed statement available about the price. That is why the drift exists, and it is why the drift is documented back to Asquith & Mullins, 1986, and why firms that issue equity underperform for years afterwards (Loughran & Ritter, 1995). This is not a new discovery. It is the oldest and least mysterious thing in this repository.

The asymmetry stated plainly. A buyback says insiders think the price is low. An issuance says they think it is high. Both are public, both are free to read, and only one of them is usually reported as good news.

three trades

What follows is the same screen applied to three real filings: the 2nd percentile outcome, the median, and the worst. Each passed identical criteria on the day it fired.

what the three have in common

Every one passed the identical screen on the day it fired. Nothing visible beforehand told them apart. Twelve pre-trade variables were tested — price, run length, RSI, volatility, volume, distance from the high, share of up days, the move on filing day — and one crossed t = 2, which is what chance produces at twelve tests. Its direction was backwards.

the instrument problem

The screen works. Nothing available to a retail account captures it. Four escape routes were tested and each one closed.

A note on the shape. The rise before the decline reads as a double top, and it is really there — a run that ends makes a high, retraces, and tries again. But 97% of signals form one and so do 91.7% of random walks at matched volatility. From the point the pattern is visible the outcome matches the base rate, and breaking the neckline was followed by less decline than an average moment. The offering is the information; the double top is what it looks like on a chart.

Stops make it worse. Every level tested lowered the mean. Of positions stopped at +50%, 58% would have ended profitable — you cut winners that went the wrong way first. 18% of stop exits gapped straight through the level.

Margin is a stop you did not choose. Of the signals that would have called you, 58% still ended in a decline. The broker closes you at a loss on positions that were right.

one refinement

The screen treats every offering alike, but the filings do not read alike. Probe 37 parsed the Use of Proceeds section of all 105 events that could be retrieved. Naming an acquisition separates nothing — 27 such filings fell 26.4% against 27.3% for the rest. Naming debt repayment does: eight filings, −59.5% mean, every one of them negative. Raising equity to pay creditors is a different act from raising it because the window is open.

Eight events is thin, and it is the only one of six categories to separate. But it points the same way as the mechanism: the more the raise looks like necessity rather than opportunism, the worse what follows.

A caution on the source. 77% of these filings say “general corporate purposes.” The section is written by counsel to preserve flexibility, not to disclose intent. Navitas said general corporate purposes in May and announced the Claros acquisition in August — no screen reading the prospectus could have known.

the ozone

The screen fires on the filing. But a 424B5 is a takedown from an existing shelf registration — without an effective S-3 a company cannot price an offering overnight. The shelf is a precondition, filed publicly in advance, and almost nobody watches it.

Probe 48 walked 900 tickers weekly. A live shelf alone does nothing: 8.15% chance of a filing within 30 days against 8.27% without one. A fresh shelf, under 90 days old, gives 11.42%. What compounds it is the run. With a shelf live: up 25% gives 12.60%, up 50% gives 17.08%, up 100% gives 23.79% — a 2.9× lift, with a median 13-day lead before the filing arrives.

One flaw, stated rather than omitted: the tickers were drawn from companies appearing in the 424B5 and S-3 records, so every one is a habitual issuer. The lifts are within-universe comparisons and stand; the absolute rates do not generalise.

and why it cannot be vented

The obvious use is to act on the warning — short during the ozone rather than after the filing, catching the collapse from higher up. Probe 49 tested 337 such entries.

Half the decline. Median −17.5% at six months against −38.7% from the filing entry. And the mean is +15.7% — the average ozone stock rose. You would be short something going up.

More than double the pain. Median adverse move of 65% against 30%. The 90th percentile is 252%, and the worst ran 4,467% — a stock that rose forty-five fold while the position was open.

And foresight does not rescue it. In the 100 cases where a 424B5 actually followed within 30 days, the mean at six months was +26.0%. Correctly anticipating the filing still lost money for a short, because the ones that kept running dominated.

The reason is plain once stated. The ozone is defined by a run in progress — that is what puts a company there. The filing is the moment the run ends. Entering before it means entering into momentum, and the position must survive the rest of the run before the thesis can pay.

EARLY AND LATE BOTH LOSE

Probe 39 found the mirror image: waiting for a double-top confirmation cut the median from −38.6% to −23.1% and raised the adverse move from 29.9% to 42.2%. Early, you pay for the rest of the run. Late, you re-base your entry lower so every bounce costs proportionally more. The filing is not a convenient entry — it is the only one, because it is the moment the information arrives.

What the ozone is for: if you hold a company sitting in it — live shelf, ran 100%, overbought — that is information about your own position. Selling into it needs no borrow, no margin and no timing precision, and the failure mode is regret rather than a call.

the one instrument left

Every route that closed was a property of the instrument, not of the signal. Shorting needs borrow and survives a margin call. Puts cost what the move is worth. Spreads do not clear the bid-ask. So probe 39 changed the instrument to the only one with no failure mode: cash.

The question a holder faces is different from the one a short faces. You already own the stock. The offering lands. Do you sell?

Across 41 signals with a confirmed double top and a full year of follow-through, selling at the confirmation and sitting out twelve months beat holding by +32.5% at the median, and was the better choice on 68% of them. At three months, +7.7% and 66%.

But the mean is +3.8% with a t of 0.24 — indistinguishable from zero at every horizon. A few missed runs pay for all the avoided declines. At the fifth percentile you are down 224% and the worst case is −400%, meaning the stock rose nearly fivefold while you sat in cash.

WHY THIS ONE SURVIVES

That tail is opportunity cost, not ruin. You still hold every dollar you sold for. The same tail on a short position is a margin call and a realised loss larger than the position itself. It is the first version of the O signal whose failure mode is regret.

Three things it does not settle. Forty-one events, and only the median is clearly positive. Tax is unmodelled and may exceed the edge. And selling a holding to buy it back lower is precisely what the disposition effect says investors fail to do — the arithmetic is the easy part.

what remains

A real, repeatable, well-explained inefficiency with no instrument a retail account can use to reach it. That is not a failure of the reasoning — it is what a genuine anomaly looks like from the outside. The returns sit where someone is paid to accept ruin.

So the desk logs them. Every O signal is recorded as it fires, with the option chain captured at that moment, building the out-of-sample evidence the backtest could not have. If real quotes on real signals turn out better than modelled, the conclusion changes. If not, it is confirmed properly rather than assumed.

SECOND ARTICLE  ·  BACKDRAFT

The door is the liquidity raise.

An offering prices 5 to 15% below the market. The stock falls far further than that. This is why.

when the shares actually arrive

Faster than most people expect, and the timing is why the drift exists.

The overnight offering, the common case. Decision made during the day. Announcement after the close, usually with the market shut. Underwriters build the book overnight, often in a few hours. Pricing before the next open, at a discount of 5 to 15%. The 424B5 files that morning, which is when it appears in the SEC index and when the monitor sees it.

The buyers can sell immediately. Shares from a shelf takedown are registered and freely tradeable on the trade date — no lockup, no restriction. Settlement is T+1 since May 2024, but the ability to sell does not wait for settlement.

So the block is in the market within roughly twelve hours of the announcement, held by institutions who bought at a discount and many of whom hedge or distribute straight away. That is the supply shock, and it lands before most holders have read the filing.

The other case never shows up. An at-the-market programme sells shares continuously through a broker, at prevailing prices, with no announcement per sale. The dilution dribbles out over months and appears only in the next 10-Q's share count. Those companies expand their float just as much and generate no O signal at all — the screen sees announcements, not dilution, and those are different things.

why a 15% discount produces a 40% fall

Because the discount isn't a price, it's an assertion — and the assertion is what moves things.

Four mechanisms stack.

ONE  ·  THE DISCOUNT IS A FLOOR, NOT A LEVEL
Institutions bought at 15% below. Every one of them is above water only while the stock holds there, and many hedge or distribute immediately. That creates persistent supply at and below the offering price, so the discount becomes the ceiling the stock has to fight rather than the bottom it settles to.
TWO  ·  DILUTION COMPOUNDS ON THE DISCOUNT
A 20% raise at 15% off means existing holders take both — a smaller slice, and the slice repriced. Those multiply rather than add.
THREE  ·  THE SIGNAL IS REREAD BACKWARDS
Before the offering, the price reflected whatever the market believed. Afterwards, everyone knows management chose to sell at that level. Holders who were confident because the stock was rising now have evidence the best-informed party disagreed. That reprices the belief, not just the shares, and belief has no floor.
FOUR  ·  THE MARGINAL BUYER DISAPPEARS
The people who would have bought at the old price just got filled at the discount instead. Demand that was going to arrive over weeks got consumed in one night.

Probe 31 measured where that ends up: −38.7% median over six months against a discount of 15%. The discount explains the first day. The other twenty-four points are the market working out what the offering told it.

the backdraft

A backdraft is a fire starved of oxygen — hot, contained, waiting. Open the door and the sudden supply is what ignites it. The heat was already there; the door just let it find fuel.

The parallel holds because in both cases the destructive event is triggered by supply arriving, not by the underlying state changing. Navitas was the same company the day before and the day after. What changed was that seventeen million shares entered a market that had been bidding against a smaller float.

And the low-float part maps too. A tightly held stock is the sealed room — price runs because there is little supply to meet demand, and the pressure builds precisely because of the constraint. The higher it runs, the more incentive to open the door.

WHERE THE METAPHOR STOPS

A backdraft is an accident, and an offering is a decision. Somebody chooses the moment, and they choose it when the room is hottest. That is the part that makes it a signal rather than a hazard.

The firefighter's rule is to vent above the fire before opening at ground level — relieve the pressure on your own terms rather than letting it choose. The market equivalent is an at-the-market programme: dilute continuously, in small amounts, so the float expands without a door ever slamming open. Which is exactly why those companies never generate an O signal.

THIRD ARTICLE  ·  TURN AND BURN

Reading vapour trails.

A dogfight, as the research reads it. The analogy is Ian's, from years of flying Ace Combat; the numbers are the corpus's.

the picture on the scope

A fighter pilot is flooded with signal and most of it is useless. The skill is telling a contact from clutter, fast. That is also what fifty-nine probes turned out to be.

AWACS ON UHF  ·  FORWARD GUIDANCE
The big picture from above: where the threats are, which way to turn. You listen, but it goes out to every squadron at once, so it gives no one an edge. It is the same information the next pilot is hearing.
THE HARD DECK  ·  THE MARGIN LINE
The altitude you may not go below. Break it and you are dead even if you win the fight. Probe 34 and the margin arithmetic: you can be right about where the enemy is going and still hit the deck before you get there.
SHORT-RANGE RADAR  ·  VOLATILITY
Close, fast, immediate. The screen lights up when it gets tight. It tells you a fight is on; it does not tell you who wins it.
IFF  ·  IDENTIFY FRIEND OR FOE
Most of what the radar paints is not hostile: ground returns, weather, friendlies. The corpus was one long IFF check, and nearly every contact came back as clutter — moving averages, the Square of Nine, planetary aspects, insider sales. Each showed up on the scope; none was a real target.
MISSILE LOCK  ·  THE O SIGNAL
Not a blip you are guessing about. A specific, identified threat with a tone. Something was filed, dated and confirmed. That is why it was the only contact worth reacting to.
BINGO FUEL  ·  PROBES 39, 57
Knowing when to break off and go home rather than stay in a fight you cannot win. Selling a position already held, into cash, which cannot call you — and doing it on the filing day, inside the firing range, not at the confirmation (probe 57).

the tail and the vortex

Two visual cues tell a pilot what an enemy aircraft is about to do, and they sit at different moments in time.

The tail is a leading cue. Rudder, elevator and ailerons must deflect before the aircraft turns. A pilot cannot bank without first moving a control surface, so aerodynamics forces the sequence and there is no hiding it. Seen early, it is real warning.

The vortex is a contemporaneous cue. Wingtip streamers appear during the hard turn, when the wing is loaded. Vivid, but they report the manoeuvre as it happens. That is volatility exactly: loud, real, and arriving mid-move. Contemporaneous correlation was strong everywhere the corpus looked. Shifted one period forward, it vanished.

WHERE REGULATION PLAYS THE ROLE OF PHYSICS

In the air, physics forces the tail to move first. In markets nothing forces the sequence, and any visible tell is acted on the moment it is spotted — so the move happens at the tell, not after it. The exceptions are where a rule forces the order. A company cannot do an overnight offering without a shelf registration already on file: the shelf is the aileron, and probe 48 found it at a 2.9× lift. The O signal works for the same reason. The SEC forces the filing into the record before the shares move.

And there are feints. A pilot who knows you read tails will jink to sell you a false one. The double top is the market's feint: everyone watches for it, and probe 38 found 91.7% of random walks draw one too.

turn and burn

The tactic: close at full speed, force the enemy to break, and read the plume before committing to your own turn.

You do not predict which way they will go. You press until they have to commit, the vapour trail appears the instant they break, and only then do you turn inside them for the shot. The plume matters because it marks a commitment that is costly to undo. An aircraft breaking hard has dumped its energy into the turn, and reversing means bleeding more. The moment you see white, the move is real.

That is the O signal. You do not guess whether a company will issue stock. You wait for the 424B5 — the plume — and it confirms a committed act that cannot be reversed. Management cannot unsell the shares. The filing is valuable for the same reason the vapour trail is: it only appears once the commitment is already made.

John Boyd, a US fighter pilot and strategist, called the loop observe, orient, decide, act. The pilot who cycles it faster wins, and forcing the enemy to react first hands you their commitment to read.

where the analogy breaks

Energy. You closed at full speed, so when they broke you had the speed to turn inside. In markets the retail trader often sees the plume perfectly and has no energy left to use it: no shares to borrow, margin forcing the exit, options priced to the move. Probes 33 to 36. The signal was clean; the aircraft could not make the turn.

Timing of the confirmation. In the air you see the plume immediately, at the instant of the break, so waiting for it costs nothing. The market version of waiting for confirmation was tested in probe 39 — hold off until the double top completes — and it made things worse: median from −38.6% to −23.1%, worst adverse move from 29.9% to 42.2%. By the time a chart confirms, much of the move has happened, you enter lower, and every bounce costs proportionally more. Probe 57 measured the same thing from the holder's side: the value of selling halves by about 31 bars after the filing. The firing range is the first week.

The quiet sky. Options are cheapest when the chart goes flat, which looks like an empty sector. But probe 26 found that when volatility sits in the bottom fifth of its range, what follows runs about 1.30× trailing, and option sellers price that in. You think you have found quiet sky; AWACS has already told every squadron the bogeys are inbound.

The plume that works in markets is the filing itself, because it shows up at the moment of commitment, like the vapour trail. Waiting for anything after it is waiting for the enemy to finish the turn.

Every Form 424B5 filed with the SEC is screened overnight. A filing is logged as an O signal only if the company also emerged from sub-$5 within the past year, ran 50%+ over twenty-one bars, shows RSI above 70, and trades at least $1M a day. The option chain is captured at the moment of detection, which is the evidence the backtest could not have.

what survived

Volatility clusters. After an unusually large bar the next bar is larger. A gap of 1.098 standard deviations in real data, against 0.320 for a random walk and 0.714 for GARCH. Documented by Engle in 1982, visible on the first night, and the only thing the following thirty-four probes confirmed.

The O signal. Uplist, run, overbought, then a secondary offering. 117 deduplicated events, −38.7% median over six months, negative in all five walk-forward windows. Real, and unreachable with any retail instrument.

Flow depth. The decline scales with how much money moves through the name: thin -49%, deep -24% at six months (rho 0.29, p 0.002). Not price, not stale marks, not dilution — thin names dilute twice as heavily and dilution still predicts nothing. The thin tercile absorbs about $1M a year at 10% of volume, which is why nobody arbitrages it away. Probe 58.

Sell on the filing day. The one route open to a holder, and its timing. Selling at the filing day's close beat holding by +37% at the median on 80% of 102 events, with a positive mean (+22%, t 3.7). Half the value is gone by about 31 bars. Probe 57.

nine errors caught in flight

A record that only shows its wins is worth less than one that shows its corrections. Probe 15 read a falling ratio as falling activity when absolute short shares had risen 35%. Probe 20 produced an 84.8% win rate that was pure lookahead. Probe 23 silently dropped the series that went to zero. Probe 31 called a −80.9% stock move a failure when it was the short's gain. Probe 35 predicted skew would be a headwind; it is a tailwind, and probe 36 then found there was no skew at all. Probe 57 first measured the seller's gain in extra shares, which is unbounded above and passed the mean by arithmetic.

the corpus

Every entry lists what it contributed, not only whether it passed. Filter by outcome or by subject; the dilution group, read in order, is the full derivation of the O signal.

THE INSTRUMENT ROOM

A projection is not a claim.

Every instrument on this page shows something the tables already state. None of them predicts anything, and the corpus says so in detail — probes 4 and 41 through 47 tested these geometries as signals and found nothing that survived a control.

They are kept because seeing is not the same as reading. A funnel widening over seventy-six years is a compounding rate you can look at. A century of rising azimuths at one latitude is a set of frequencies you can watch turn. The distinction the whole repository rests on is that a display choice asserts nothing, and the moment one is used to generate levels it becomes a claim and has to be tested. Probes 46 and 47 tested exactly that and found hit rate tracking level density at r = 0.982.

This page will take more instruments as they are built.

Full screen, outside the desk: the orrery  ·  the spiral in 3D  ·  the Giza clock, which is the construction probe 41 actually tested — Jupiter's ray striking a course of stone, that course seeding a Square of Nine, and the histogram of how far its levels missed.

I

an esoteric appendix

W.D. Gann worked with planetary position, and this repository began as a test of his geometry. Probes 4, and 41 through 47, closed that thread: aspects, rising azimuth at Giza, pairwise proximity across seven bodies, and geomagnetic storms measured since 1932. All null — the last at 1.54 basis points against a 2 to 5 basis point round trip.

The construction that prompted it: Jupiter rises over the plateau, the ray crosses the pyramid and strikes a course of stone, and that course seeds a Square of Nine which generates price levels. Built and run, its median miss to the nearest level was 3.68% against 3.46% for the same Square seeded by a randomly chosen course — the 60.3rd percentile of two thousand draws, marginally worse than picking the starting stone by chance. Every element downstream of the azimuth is a deterministic function of it, so the whole apparatus carries exactly what the azimuth carries, and probe 41 measured that at r = 0.021 after detrending.

What follows is not that test. It is the observatory itself: a century of rising azimuths over the Great Pyramid for seven bodies, each hand on and off at a click. It is here because it is a fine thing to look at.

Colour follows frequency: the Moon completes a cycle in 27 days and is drawn white; Saturn takes 29.5 years and is drawn deep red. Fast at the blue end, slow at the red, the way a spectrum runs. Mars is the reference hand — at 687 days it moves visibly across a few months without aliasing the way the Moon does at this sampling, so it is the one you can read by eye. Its recent path trails behind it on the rim.

Distance from the centre is price. Angle is time. One full turn equals the period you choose, so every point on the same ray shares the same phase — at 365 days every January lies on one line, and a season can be read radially instead of hunted along an axis.

Nothing here is fitted and nothing is predicted. It is the same series in polar coordinates, and the only claim made for it is that phase is easier to see when it is an angle. Probes 4, 46 and 47 tested spiral constructions as sources of price levels and found hit rate tracking level coverage at r = 0.982 and r = 0.955, every one below its own shuffled null. Drawing price on a spiral is a display choice; deriving levels from one is not supported.

WHAT THE RAYS MEAN

NOTE

A rising market spirals outward. A falling one winds in. Where the curve crosses its own previous turn, price has returned to a level it held one period earlier — which is the thing a linear chart makes you measure and this one shows.

the same thing in three dimensions

Flat, the chart has one radius and must spend it on either price or time. With a third axis it keeps both: the wind carries time and phase, and height carries price. Seventy-six years of the index becomes a funnel, widening because log height makes equal distances equal percentages — the flare is the compounding, drawn rather than computed.

Pick a reference and both series are clipped to their overlap and indexed to 100, so the gap between the two lines at any turn is relative performance at that moment. Press play and they grow together from the origin.

ask the corpus

Fifty-nine pre-registered experiments, searchable. Type a question or an idea and this returns the sections of the actual run files that bear on it, ranked, with their numbers intact. It retrieves; it does not paraphrase, so it cannot invent a finding. If nothing comes back, nothing was tested — which is a finding of a kind.

Numbers match literally: searching 38.7 or t=-5.39 finds the passage that states it, and probe 31 goes straight there.

HOW IT RANKS

Terms are matched against each section's text, its probe title and its heading, with rarer words weighted more heavily and phrase matches scored above scattered ones. At most three sections per probe, so one cannot fill the page. Every figure shown is the probe's own output, and each probe's pass rules were written before its results were read.

Where does it stand?

Type a ticker. This runs the probe-31 screen and the probe-48 ozone state against live prices and tells you which conditions hold right now.

It reports state, not advice. A signal firing is not a recommendation to act — probes 33 to 36 closed every instrument, and probe 49 showed that acting earlier is worse. What this answers is the narrow question the corpus can actually answer: do the conditions hold, and what did that mean across 117 events?

WHAT IT CHECKS AND WHAT IT CANNOT

Prices come live from the monitor's proxy. The five price conditions — prior-year low under $5, current price over $5, 21-bar run, RSI(14), median daily dollar volume — are computed here and are exact. The sixth condition, a 424B5 on file, is not checked in the browser; that is the monitor's nightly job, and a ticker showing five of six is a candidate rather than a signal. Shelf status is likewise not fetched here. Where an option chain is available it is pulled live from CBOE.
