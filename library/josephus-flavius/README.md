# Flavius Josephus — Reading Room

Three of the four surviving works, about 825,000 words. Everything here is public domain:
Josephus died around 100 CE and the translation dates from 1736.

Read this page before citing anything. Josephus is unusually easy to acquire and unusually
easy to misuse.

**Missing:** the *Life*, the short autobiographical sequel appended to the *Antiquities* in the
manuscripts. It is on Project Gutenberg and has not yet been taken. The four works together are
the complete surviving corpus — there is no good reason to think Josephus published anything
else.

## Three cautions

**1. The translation is old and not good.** William Whiston's 1736 version is the only complete
public-domain English Josephus, which is why it is here. It is also the one most English readers
have ever encountered. Its weaknesses are well documented: Whiston had no access to a sound
Greek text, his rendering is frequently at fault, and he cared little for style. Shilleto's
1889-90 revision is better but appeared just before two modern critical editions of the Greek
and is itself now dated.

Treat this text as a serviceable reading copy, not as an authority on wording.

**2. The author is a participant, not an observer.** Josephus commanded Jewish forces in
Galilee, surrendered to Vespasian, took his patron's family name, and wrote his account of the
war in Rome under Flavian protection. He is simultaneously the indispensable source for
first-century Judaea and a man with pressing reasons to shape how that period was remembered.

**3. Two passages are disputed, and they are flagged inside the text.** See below.

## The Jesus passages

`01-antiquities.md` contains the two references to Jesus. Both carry a warning block at the
passage itself, so a reader arriving mid-book cannot miss the issue.

**Ant. XVIII.3.3 — the Testimonium Flavianum.** The most contested sentence in ancient
historiography, argued over since the 16th century. Opinion divides three ways: wholly
authentic (a small minority), wholly a later Christian interpolation, or — the majority view —
an authentic core into which Christian copyists inserted confessional phrases that a
non-Christian Jew would not have written.

**Ant. XX.9.1 — the reference to the brother of Jesus.** Generally regarded as being on firmer
ground: incidental, unadorned, and reading as Josephus rather than as a copyist. Still disputed
by some.

**These two do not stand or fall together.** Weigh them separately. A room that presents
Whiston's Josephus without flagging them would be reproducing contested text as settled — and
Whiston, an 18th-century clergyman, translated the Testimonium as genuine and annotated in its
defence. His footnotes at that point are advocacy, not apparatus.

## A layering worth knowing

The Greek *Jewish War* is itself Josephus's own translation of a lost first draft written in his
native Aramaic. `02-wars-of-the-jews.md` is therefore Whiston's 1736 English of Josephus's Greek
of his own vanished Aramaic — three removes, with the innermost layer gone.
