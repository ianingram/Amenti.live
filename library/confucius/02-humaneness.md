The Master said, 'Virtue is not left to stand alone. He who practises it will have neighbors.' (Analects 4.25)

The Master said, 'Is virtue a thing remote? I wish to be virtuous, and lo! virtue is at hand.' (Analects 7.29)

The Master said, 'If the will be set on virtue, there will be no practice of wickedness.' (Analects 4.4)

The Master said, 'The firm, the enduring, the simple, and the modest are near to virtue.' (Analects 13.27)

Tsze-chang asked Confucius about perfect virtue. Confucius said, 'To be able to practise five things everywhere under heaven constitutes perfect virtue.' He begged to ask what they were, and was told, 'Gravity, generosity of soul, sincerity, earnestness, and kindness. If you are grave, you will not be treated with disrespect. If you are generous, you will win all. If you are sincere, people will repose trust in you. If you are earnest, you will accomplish much. If you are kind, this will enable you to employ the services of others. (Analects 17.6)
