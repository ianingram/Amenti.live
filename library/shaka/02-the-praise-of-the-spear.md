Before him a man threw his spear and then stood empty, and having thrown it he was only a man with a shield, waiting. He said: why do you give your weapon to the enemy before the fighting starts? So he took the long shaft and broke it short, and he made the blade broad, and he called it by the sound it makes coming out — *iklwa*. It is not thrown. It is not lent. It stays in the hand and it goes in underneath, past the rim of the shield, and the shield that was made to stop a thing coming through the air is no use against a thing that comes from below. He made them leave their sandals so their feet would learn the ground, and he made them run until running was not a hardship but a way of arriving. The spear did not win the wars by itself. The spear only meant that when two men met, one of them still had his weapon.
