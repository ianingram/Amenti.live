*The mind forms the thought, then gives it voice.*

This is the product thesis of the Amenti Interface — the reasoning behind a Library of Legends that does not merely answer, but answers in its own voice. It is built in the image of a mind.

**The thesis — one mind, three faculties, bound into a single act**

A brain does not think and speak at once with a single organ. It composes a thought in one faculty, lends it words in another, and keeps what it has made in a third. These are distinct powers — sequenced, not braided — and meaning travels cleanly between them.

The Amenti Neural Interface is built in that image. Not as metaphor for a brochure, but as architecture. Where a lesser system asks one model to think and speak in the same tangled breath, Amenti separates the faculties and lets each be itself. The result is an interface that behaves the way a mind does: it forms the meaning first, then gives it voice.

Three faculties carry the work:

- **Cognition** — the mind, served by Claude. It forms meaning: the figure reasons in the first person and composes the thought — the reply, the article, the dispatch. Nothing is spoken until something is meant.
- **Articulation** — the voice, served by Gemini. It renders speech: the formed thought is given a voice, directed rather than cloned — a timbre, an accent, a manner of delivery. Speech arrives only after thought.
- **Memory** — the record, held in the cloud store. It keeps what is made: composed content lives apart from the surface, where the page cannot corrupt it. The library remembers across every visit.

**The principle — form the meaning, then give it voice**

This is the single idea the whole system repeats at every scale. The brain receives content, then loads speech. First the thought is formed; then it is rendered. We did not invent the sequence — we built a system in its image.

Sequencing is what makes the interface honest. A figure cannot speak a thing it has not first meant, and so the voice is never a performance laid over emptiness — it is the delivery of a thought that genuinely exists. Each faculty improves on its own clock: the voice can deepen without disturbing cognition, the mind can grow without retuning the voice. They meet only at a clean seam.

*Cognition and articulation are different faculties. Do not braid them into one tangled pipeline. Sequence them — each served by what it is best at.*

**Cognition — Claude forms the thought**

Cognition is the figure thinking. Every spoken reply, every article, every dispatch begins here: Claude reasons in the figure's persona and composes meaning in the first person. Nothing is rendered as speech until something has been meant.

The surface never calls the cognition model directly. It calls the nervous system, which carries the persona and the conversation to Claude and returns the formed thought. The persona is held as the system instruction; the exchange is the running conversation. Cognition owns one job and does it completely — it produces text, never audio. A request enters, the nervous system routes it to the mind, and the formed reply returns to the surface: text in, meaning out, with speech reserved for a later faculty.

**Articulation — Gemini gives it voice**

Articulation takes the formed thought and renders it as speech. The discovery that made this tractable: a figure's voice does not need to be cloned — it needs to be directed. Gemini is an actor that takes stage direction, and direction is prose, which is exactly what this project is made of.

A voice is composed from the figure's own record. Gender selects the base timbre. The register — the surface's mode — joined with the accent and the manner of delivery becomes a single natural-language style direction. That direction and the base voice ride one request to the voice route of the nervous system, and the formed thought returns as audio. The hard problem of per-figure voice models collapses into the easy one of per-figure prose.

**Memory — the library keeps what it makes**

A library of legends needs things for the legends to say beyond live chat — daily writings, a newsroom, a periodical. Rather than author all of this by hand, Amenti became a publisher: an engine that writes once, keeps the result, and serves it forever. The keeping happens in memory, a store the display code cannot reach.

This yields a property worth more than its elegance: content lives apart from display. A surface can break entirely while every composed article sits untouched and safe. You cannot lose the content by breaking the page. Memory is addressed by a locked scheme, and it answers before the mind is ever troubled to compose again — a request checks the store first; on a hit it serves what is kept, and only on a miss does the mind compose, after which the result is committed and every future reader receives the cache.

**The nervous system — one endpoint routes the three**

The principle is not a slogan; it is the shape of the build. Every surface of Amenti speaks to a single worker — the nervous system — which routes each signal to the faculty it belongs to. Thought to the mind, voice to the voice, the record to memory. The keys that unlock cognition and articulation live here as secrets, and nowhere else.

The handler is short on purpose. The nervous system carries signals; it does not think or speak. Each route hands off to the one faculty that owns the work, and the surface that calls in never sees the secrets that lie behind it.

**Serving the surface**

Amenti speaks in several places, and they are not built the same way — different roster loaders, different readers — yet they share one composition and one endpoint. A surface asks the nervous system; behind it, the mind composes, the voice renders, and memory remembers. To the surface, it is a single conversation, and it never needs to know which faculty answered.

The whole act, from the surface's side, is three steps in sequence: the mind forms the thought, the voice is composed from the figure's own record, and the same act continues into speech. Long-form reading is the same act one layer down — the brain does not finish an entire thought before it begins to speak, so the surface splits the text, renders each part in turn, and plays them gap-free as they arrive. Speech is streamed because thought is chunked. The principle holds at every scale.

*The library thinks — and then it speaks. The whole system is one idea repeated at every scale: form the meaning, then give it voice.*
