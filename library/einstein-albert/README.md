# Albert Einstein — Reading Room

Seven primary texts, roughly 200,000 words, every one of them in Einstein's own words or an
authorised translation of them. No paraphrase, no summary standing in for a source.

This page explains what is here, what is deliberately absent, and where the room is knowingly
imperfect. Read it before citing anything.

## The room's boundary

**This is the physicist. It is not the man.**

Almost everything Einstein wrote after 1929 remains under copyright: the correspondence, the
1939 letter to Roosevelt, *The World As I See It*, the essays on pacifism and Zionism, the
Russell–Einstein Manifesto, the exchanges with Born. None of it can be held here, and that will
not change for decades.

So a reader who took this room as a portrait would meet a man who thought about measuring-rods
and clocks and apparently nothing else. That is a distortion produced by copyright law, not by
Einstein. The absence is stated here rather than left to be discovered, because an unstated gap
is a portrait with the politics quietly cropped out.

The Princeton *Digital Einstein Papers* hold the full documentary record and are freely
readable online. `08-linkouts.md` points there.

## Three known problems

Each is recorded in the file it affects, not only here.

**1. The German text may be incomplete.** `02-...-1917.md` runs about 19,300 words against the
English 33,500. It breaks off near the English Chapter XXV; Part III and the appendices are
absent. Two possible explanations — that it is the shorter 1917 first printing, before those
chapters were added, or that the scan is partial — and they have not been distinguished.

Consequence: **do not read absence in the German as evidence about Einstein.** If a passage is
missing there, check the English before concluding anything.

If it turns out to be the 1917 printing, that is a better outcome than a clean parallel text:
the two files would then show the book growing between 1917 and 1920, the cosmological chapters
arriving as Einstein turned the theory on the universe itself.

**2. `03-principle-of-relativity.md` is the wrong translation for primary use.** It is the
Calcutta 1920 edition by Meghnad Saha and Satyendranath Bose, not the 1923 Methuen line. The
Saha rendering abridges: the derivation in §3 is shortened, a formula in §10 silently corrected,
and Einstein's closing thanks to Michele Besso dropped entirely.

It is kept anyway, and labelled, because it is a real document in its own right — how relativity
first reached an Indian scientific readership, translated by two physicists who went on to major
work of their own. Reception history, not primary text. Use `07-1905-papers.md` for the 1905
paper.

**3. Page 22 of the 1905 paper failed to convert.** The gap is marked inline. It carries the
close of the electrodynamical section and, in print, the Besso acknowledgement — the very
passage that made this translation preferable to Saha's. Recoverable from the fourmilab PDF;
not yet recovered.

## On editions

The single most useful thing learned in building this room: **two productions of the same book
by the same translator can differ in fidelity, apparatus, and markup all at once, and the better
one on one axis is not the better one on the others.**

`01-relativity.md` and `01a-relativity-3rd-ed.md` are both Lawson's authorised translation.
They differ in three ways:

- `01a` encodes mathematics as LaTeX; `01` renders it as unusable prose descriptions.
- `01a` keeps Einstein's own example — Potsdamer Platz, supplemented by Lawson with Trafalgar
  Square. `01`'s transcribers substituted Times Square, a third layer of localisation on one
  place-name.
- `01` carries Appendix IV; `01a` stops at III.

Neither is redundant. **Quote Chapter II from `01a`. Take Appendix IV from `01`.**

Appendix V of *Relativity* is absent from both and always will be: Einstein added it at the
fifteenth reprinting, and it remains in copyright.

## Provenance

Every text came from Project Gutenberg or the Internet Archive, downloaded directly and
converted from EPUB. Each file records its source, edition, translator, and production notes in
its own header. Nothing here was retyped, reconstructed, or filled in from memory — where a
source was damaged or short, the file says so instead.
