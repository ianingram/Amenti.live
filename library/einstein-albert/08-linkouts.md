# Link-outs — material not ingested

Everything here stays a link. Either it isn't Einstein's voice, or it isn't free.

## Contemporary reception (public domain, Gutenberg — ingest only if the room wants a reception shelf)

- **J. Malcolm Bird**, *Einstein's Theories of Relativity and Gravitation* — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/63372
- **Edwin E. Slosson**, *Easy Lessons in Einstein* — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/75807
- **Erwin Freundlich**, *The Foundations of Einstein's Theory of Gravitation* — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/70793
- **Upton Sinclair**, *Mental Radio* — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/63693
  (Einstein wrote a preface to the German edition; the book is Sinclair's, on telepathy. Curiosity, not source.)

These are the 1920s public metabolising him in real time. Primary *reception*, not primary
Einstein — a different shelf, and it should be labelled as one.

## Archive — free to read, not free to ingest

## Walled — the gap in the room

Substantially all of the post-1929 writing remains in copyright:

- the correspondence, in general
- the 1939 letter to Roosevelt
- *The World As I See It* (1934)
- the pacifist and political essays
- the 1955 Russell–Einstein Manifesto
- the Born correspondence

## Standing decision required

The buildable room is almost purely scientific. The man who wrote to Roosevelt and the man who
wrote on pacifism, Zionism, and war are behind a wall that does not lift for decades.

The room should say so on its own front page rather than presenting the physicist as though he
were the whole figure. A stated absence is honest; an unstated one is a portrait with the
politics quietly cropped out.
