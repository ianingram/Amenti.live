# William Shakespeare — Reading Room

Two texts, about 1.8 million words between them. Everything here is public domain; Shakespeare
died in 1616 and the First Folio was printed in 1623.

This page explains what is here and why there are two files rather than one. Read it before
citing anything.

## Why two files

**There is no manuscript.** Not one authorial holograph of any Shakespeare play survives.
Everything descends from printed books, and the printed books disagree.

For roughly twenty plays — including *The Tempest*, *Macbeth*, and *Twelfth Night* — the 1623
Folio is the earliest text of any kind. For others there are competing quarto printings, and
the disagreements are not trivial: *Hamlet* and *King Lear* differ enough between witnesses
that the *Lear* texts are arguably two different plays.

Every modern reading edition is therefore an editor's construction, assembled by choosing among
witnesses. That is honest scholarship, but it is not the same as a source.

So: **`01` is the readable descendant. `02` is what it descends from.** Read from the first;
check contested passages against the second. A room holding only `01` would present editorial
judgement as though it were Shakespeare's.

## One thing worth seeing

The Folio opens with its own catalogue of the plays it contains, grouped as Comedies, Histories,
and Tragedies. *Troilus and Cressida* does not appear in that list — but it is printed in the
book. The compilers' own table of contents fails to account for one of their own plays.

The first collected Shakespeare disagrees with itself about what it is. That is the kind of
thing a reading room should show rather than tidy away.

## What is flagged, not solved

**Attribution.** *The Two Noble Kinsmen* is a collaboration with John Fletcher. *The Passionate
Pilgrim* is a 1599 miscellany published under Shakespeare's name whose contents are largely by
other people. Both sit inside `01` at present, unseparated. Whether they move to a `disputed/`
shelf is undecided.

**Rendering.** Both files exceed GitHub's display limit of about 1 MB. They commit and download
correctly but will not show in the browser.

**Splitting.** Breaking these into per-play files would fix the rendering problem and let each
play carry its own note on which witnesses disagree. Recommended, not yet done. The Folio makes
this harder than it sounds: Gutenberg's conversion has no per-play markup at all, so boundaries
have to be detected from the text itself.

## Provenance

Both files came from Project Gutenberg, downloaded directly and converted from EPUB on disk.
Gutenberg's own guide advises preferring these editions over their 1100 and 2200 series, which
are less well maintained duplicates. Nothing here was retyped or reconstructed.
