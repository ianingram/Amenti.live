To know your own way you must know the others, so this book is about the other schools, and I will not be polite about them. Some make a virtue of the long sword and say reach decides everything. But in a narrow room, in a crowd, on a stair, reach is a burden. Some make a virtue of the short sword and speak of darting in; that is a plan that depends on the other man being slow. Some teach great strength in the grip, some teach elaborate footwork, some sell a hundred named stances and a secret entrance to the school. A hundred stances is a hundred things to be caught between. These are not ways of strategy. They are ways of teaching, which is a different trade with different rewards — a school that promises a secret can charge for it. Take what is true from any of them. Distrust anything that must be paid for twice.
