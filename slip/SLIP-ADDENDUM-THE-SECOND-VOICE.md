## THE FINDING THAT PRODUCED THESE

Raised by the captain, who has been on the other side of a conversation with a
figure and the assistant has not:

> **One-on-one is intense, and intensity is a demand.** A stranger arrives, is
> immediately the sole participant, is expected to lead, and a dead man is
> waiting on them. It is the most novel thing the site does and it is also the
> steepest door it offers.
>
> **Two figures lowers the stakes**, because the conversation exists without the
> reader. They can listen first and enter when they have something. And two
> voices collide in ways neither reaches alone.

Underneath it, a second finding about the site as a whole: **Amenti opens on a
roster of names.** A catalogue is a card index, not an invitation — it asks who
you have heard of rather than what you want. These two moves are the same
observation from two ends: **the way in is wrong, and the first thing behind it
is harder than it needs to be.**

## MOVE · The second voice — *enter the conversation*

Two figures on one question, with the reader free to join. **A SCENE, NOT A
ROOM**: it starts, it has a shape, and it ends.

**The counsel already does most of this.** It stages a figure into a role, it
carries its own register, and the Turn is already the rolling summary. A second
voice is a change of STAGING, not a new system — which is the reframe that makes
this cheap enough to try rather than a quarter's work.

**Test before building.** Two figures, one question, read what comes out. It
costs about **$0.28 for a ten-turn scene** and it answers what no amount of
design conversation will: whether two voices is genuinely easier and funnier, or
merely noisier. **Nothing should be built until that has been read.**

- **Unblocks:** a gentler first encounter than the empty prompt, and the pairings
  the library can make that nothing else can — Caesar and Jesus on authority,
  where Caesar speaks as **Pontifex Maximus**, which he held from 63 BC for the
  rest of his life. Nobody clicks Caesar to ask about religion, and that is
  exactly the point.
- **Acceptance test:** run one scene by hand and read it beside a one-on-one
  transcript. If it is not plainly better, this move closes there and costs two
  dollars.

### What is not decided

- **Turn-taking.** Round-robin is mechanical and shows within a minute. Letting a
  model choose the next speaker is another call per turn. The doctrine governs
  one figure and one reader; it says nothing about two figures who might
  interrupt.
- **Whether §4.6 memory applies.** A figure who remembers the reader in a room
  with a figure who does not is a strange asymmetry, and the no-leakage rule was
  written for one soul at a time.
- **THE THREE-FIGURE ROOM IS A DIFFERENT PRODUCT AND IS NOT THIS MOVE.**
  Persistent rooms other readers can join bring presence, moderation, and a room
  that **spends money while empty**. Three figures for ten turns is $0.41 and
  runs whether anyone is there or not. It is the first idea in this session that
  does not fit inside the existing economics.

## MOVE · The way in — *need a tutor?*

A page that asks what a reader WANTS rather than who they have heard of. Cards —
Newton, Galileo, and whoever else the roster puts there — under categories.
*Join the debate. Enter the conversation.*

**The categories should be tags on figures, not curated lists.** A hand-written
"religion" list is Augustine, Aquinas, Luther — the obvious ones. **Caesar never
makes it**, because whoever writes the list thinks of him as a general. Tags let
the roster surprise; a list can only ever return what its author already knew.

**And the tag is what a figure can SPEAK TO, not what they were.** The same
distinction the eighteen-term type vocabulary drew for the works. The caution is
the corpus's own: a surprising pairing must be TRUE. Caesar on Roman religion is
grounded; Caesar on faith in general is a figure stretched to fill a slot, which
is the thing the whole primary-source moat exists to refuse.

**The cards come from the roster, never typed.** A page hardcoding six figures
goes stale the day a seventh belongs there.

- **Unblocks:** a door for a stranger who wants something and does not yet have a
  name in mind — and, because each category is its own surface carrying its own
  `?via=`, the first real answer to which kind of question brings people in.
- **Acceptance test:** open it cold, pick a category, land in a conversation
  without having typed a name. Then `GET /visits` shows the category in `via`.

### And the advertising widget is a SLICE of this page, not a separate build

Same categories, same cards, same roster source — an excerpt, embeddable. Built
that way the ad cannot promise what the site does not deliver, and neither goes
stale when the roster changes. Built the other way round you get a marketing
artifact and a site that still opens on a list of names.

**A widget runs on somebody else's origin**, and the proxy's allowlist exists to
stop a hostile site spending the keys from a visitor's browser. So the widget
calls NOTHING: cards and a link out. The conversation happens on Amenti's ground.
For an ad unit that is the correct answer anyway.

## WHAT THESE TWO SHARE

**The second voice is the better thing to have behind the door.** If the scene
works, the way-in page has something to offer that a single card cannot — *enter
the conversation* is a better invitation than *talk to Newton*, and it is a
truer description of what is on the other side.

So the order is: **test the scene first.** It is two dollars and an afternoon,
and it decides what the page is advertising.

---

## ALSO SHELVED, AND WHY — letters from figures

Considered the same night and **parked deliberately**, recorded so it is not
rediscovered as new.

A letter from a soul — in an on-platform inbox, or by email with the letter kept
on the platform and the mail a bare notification — is a good idea with a fatal
adjacency: **it competes with the conversation for the same attention.** Worse,
it is EASIER. A reader who came to talk reads the letter instead, feels they have
had the encounter, and leaves. It would intercept the product.

It is also a solution to a retention problem **nobody has measured**. The visit
reading went live 28 Aug with one day of data. If most visits are two minutes and
nobody returns, a letter is a re-engagement tool. If people talk at length and
come back unprompted, it is correspondence. Those are different products and the
readings decide which.

**Revisit when the visit reading has a week behind it. Not before.**
